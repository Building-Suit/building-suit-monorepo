## 1. Executive Summary

### 1.1 Business Purpose

Building Suit is a subscription-based digital operating platform for self-managed residential buildings. It is intended to replace informal building administration through WhatsApp, paper records, spreadsheets, and unstructured transfers with a transparent and repeatable operating model.

The initial business focuses on buildings managed by a building administrator, residents' association, or similar local representative rather than by a professional property-management company. The platform creates business value by reducing administrative burden, improving financial transparency, documenting building activity, and increasing resident participation.

Building Suit is the first product portal in a wider multi-portal platform strategy. Shop Suit, Business Suit, and City Suit are future, separate business lines. They may share a global identity and platform core but must not be combined into one monolithic customer portal.

### 1.2 Business Opportunity

The target market is underserved because current alternatives generally fall into three categories:

- Informal tools such as WhatsApp, Instapay transfers notebooks, spreadsheets, and manual payment records.
- Property-management systems focused on landlords, leases, or professional property managers.
- Compound-management systems designed for large developers and gated communities.

Building Suit targets the gap between these categories: self-managed, multi-unit residential buildings that need operational structure but do not require a large enterprise property-management system.

### 1.3 Business Value Proposition

| Stakeholder | Business Value |
|---|---|
| Building administrator | Easier collection tracking, reduced disputes, documented expenses, structured communication, and less operational pressure. |
| Resident | Greater trust through financial visibility, documented issues, announcements, and structured participation in decisions. |
| Building community | A persistent operational history that remains available when administrators or residents change. |
| Future service provider | Future value only if provider scope is reintroduced after MVP. |
| Building Suit business | Recurring subscription revenue, increasing retention through accumulated operational history, and a foundation for future portal revenue. |

### 1.4 Strategic Positioning

Building Suit must be positioned as:

- A digital operational-control layer for residential buildings.
- A transparency and accountability system, not a social network.
- A mobile-first product suited to existing Egyptian user behavior.
- A building-by-building adoption product led initially by administrators.
- A scalable platform foundation for future, independently operated portals.

### 1.5 Business Outcomes

The business intends to achieve the following outcomes:

1. Validate that self-managed residential buildings will adopt a structured digital operating system.
2. Prove that administrators will initiate onboarding and invite residents.
3. Demonstrate measurable improvements in financial transparency and operational documentation.
4. Validate a recurring subscription model tied to active Units with a per-Building minimum fee.
5. Establish retention through operational history and resident participation.
6. Create neighborhood-level adoption density that lowers future acquisition costs.
7. Establish a platform foundation capable of supporting separate future portals.

## 2. Business Context and Problem Definition

### 2.1 Current-State Business Process

Residential building administration is commonly performed through a combination of:

- WhatsApp groups for announcements, issues, and discussions.
- Cash, Instapay and other manual methods for resident payments.
- Paper notebooks and spreadsheets for charges and expenses.
- Informal verbal agreements for decisions.
- Individual administrator knowledge rather than a persistent building record.

This operating model creates a dependency on the current administrator without giving the building a reliable operational system.

### 2.2 Business Problems

| Problem ID | Business Problem | Business Impact |
|---|---|---|
| BP-01 | Payments and expenses are recorded manually or inconsistently. | Disputes, collection delays, and financial mistrust. |
| BP-02 | Residents cannot easily verify building financial activity. | Reduced confidence in administration and lower willingness to pay. |
| BP-03 | Issues and maintenance discussions are mixed into messaging threads. | Poor follow-up, lost context, and repeated problems. |
| BP-04 | Building decisions lack a structured and legitimate participation process. | Disputed decisions and low resident engagement. |
| BP-05 | Unit, owner, tenant, and resident records are not maintained centrally. | Inaccurate access, unclear responsibility, and difficult transitions. |
| BP-06 | Announcements and urgent notices have no reliable read history. | Important information may be missed or disputed. |
| BP-07 | Administrator knowledge is not transferred when responsibility changes. | Operational history is lost and the next administrator starts again manually. |
| BP-08 | Local service-provider discovery depends on informal recommendations. | Deferred outside MVP; revisit only if provider scope is reintroduced later. |
| BP-09 | Existing products are poorly aligned with self-managed residential buildings. | The target market continues using informal tools. |

### 2.3 Root Causes

- The market lacks a simple, locally appropriate operating system for self-managed buildings.
- Existing informal tools solve communication or money transfer but do not solve administration.
- Building administration is treated as a personal responsibility rather than a repeatable operating process.
- Adoption requires trust and direct administrator engagement.
- Resident participation is difficult unless a meaningful portion of the building joins.

### 2.4 Cost of Inaction

Without Building Suit or an equivalent operating model:

- Buildings continue to experience recurring collection disputes and unclear balances.
- Administrators remain overloaded and exposed to accusations or misunderstandings.
- Decisions continue without reliable participation records.
- Operational history remains fragmented and difficult to audit.
- New administrators inherit incomplete information.
- The business opportunity remains open to competitors that can establish local trust and density first.

## 4. Market, Customer Segments, and Personas

### 4.1 Initial Geographic Market

The initial geographic strategy is Cairo and Giza, with emphasis on concentrated neighborhoods where direct acquisition and referrals can create local density.

The supplied presentation uses the following market assumptions:

| Market Layer | Planning Assumption |
|---|---|
| Target-area residential buildings | Approximately 482,000 |
| Target-area multi-unit residential buildings | Approximately 241,000 |
| Average building size | Approximately 24 units |
| Serviceable self-managed buildings | Approximately 168,000-170,000 |
| Three-year target | 1,000 buildings |

The market analysis references CAPMAS housing data and internal filtering assumptions. These figures require independent business validation before external publication.

### 4.2 Ideal Customer Profile

The initial ideal customer profile is:

- A self-managed, multi-unit residential building.
- Located in Cairo, Giza, or a selected nearby urban cluster.
- Managed by a building administrator or residents' association.
- Currently using WhatsApp, paper records, Excel, cash, Instapay or other manual payment records.
- Experiencing recurring collection, transparency, communication, or decision-making problems.
- Able to identify an administrator willing to lead onboarding.
- Large enough to benefit from structure but not served by an enterprise compound-management system.

Mixed-use buildings may be strategically attractive because they can support later Shop Suit relationships, but mixed-use targeting must not cause Shop Suit functionality to be added to the Building Suit portal.

### 4.3 Business Personas

| Persona | Business Need | Role in Adoption | Business Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Building Administrator | Reduce operational effort and improve credibility | Primary buyer, champion, and initial operator | Fewer disputes, clearer records, easier recurring administration |

| Tenant | Participate in the building associated with the occupied unit | Active resident with unit voting priority | Representation in current building decisions |
| Owner | Maintain visibility and participate when eligible | Resident and unit stakeholder | Financial and operational awareness |
| Future Service Provider | Reach nearby potential customers if provider scope is reintroduced later | Future/Post-MVP participant only | Requires separate approval before any MVP actor, journey, permission, API, or table becomes active |
| Founder / Sales Operator | Acquire and activate early buildings | Initial sales, onboarding, and feedback owner | Low-cost learning and initial market validation |
| Customer Support / Operations | Help buildings complete setup and resolve issues | Retention and service owner | Lower churn and consistent customer outcomes |
| Finance Operations | Manage subscription collection and reconciliation | Revenue-control owner | Accurate recurring revenue and reduced leakage |
| Platform Operator / Super-Admin | Monitor and manage all portals, buildings, subscriptions, users, and platform configuration from a privileged internal panel | Internal operations and oversight owner | Platform-wide visibility and control; all actions audited; never accessible to regular portal users |
| Future Shop Owner / Staff | Operate a small commercial business | Future Shop Suit customer | Requires a separate Shop Suit BRD |
| Future Business or City Operator | Manage enterprise or multi-building domains | Future portal customer | Requires separate portal-specific BRDs |

### 4.4 Buyer, User, and Payer

For the initial Building Suit business model:

- **Primary buyer/champion:** Building administrator.
- **Primary operational users:** Building administrator and residents.
- **Subscription owner:** Building.
- **Payer:** Building administrator on behalf of the building.
- **Beneficiaries:** Administrator, residents, and the building community.

Residents may view subscription status but are not the primary subscription payer in the documented Phase 1 model.

## 5. Business Scope

### 5.1 In-Scope Business Capabilities

The initial Building Suit business must support:

- Administrator-led or company-assisted building creation and setup.
- Unit and resident registration, approval, and lifecycle management.
- Owner and tenant distinction.
- Active-unit measurement.
- Maintenance-fee and recurring-charge configuration.
- Manual payment and expense recording.
- Building-level and unit-level financial transparency.
- Structured issues and issue history.
- Administrator announcements and read tracking.
- Unit-based governance and voting.
- Basic building notifications.
- Permanent manual payment recording for resident payments, even after Paymob activation.
- Paymob activation preparation for both resident-to-building collection and Building Suit SaaS subscription billing.
- Building subscription management and finance-feature locking.
- Building-level reporting sufficient to support transparency and subscription operations.

### 5.2 Out-of-Scope for Initial Launch

- A full marketplace with booking, ordering, settlement, or dispute management.
- Service Provider Directory, provider profile creation/editing, provider discovery/contact journeys, provider permissions, provider APIs/tables/security rules, and provider moderation.
- Property sales, rental, and listing marketplace.
- Events and community fundraising.
- Smart or monetized notifications.
- Online payment-gateway activation before Paymob merchant, legal/compliance, technical, reconciliation, support, and runbook readiness is approved.
- Shop Suit product workflows.
- Business Suit product workflows.
- City Suit or compound-management workflows.
- Advanced multi-administrator models.
- Broad external integrations.
- Unapproved cross-portal data sharing.

### 5.3 Multi-Portal Business Boundary

The platform strategy includes multiple business portals, but each portal must have an independent customer proposition, scope, operating model, pricing model, roadmap, and business approval.

| Portal | Business Role | Initial Status |
|---|---|---|
| Building Suit | Residential-building operational infrastructure | Initial active business |
| Shop Suit | Small-shop operations and future local-commerce connection | Future separate business line |
| Business Suit | Enterprise or business-building operations | Future separate business line |
| City Suit | Compound, multi-building, or city-level operations | Future separate business line |
| Super-Admin | Internal owner/operator control panel for onboarding requests, internal reporting, user management, subscription management, support operations, platform-controlled configuration, trial policy configuration, pricing policy configuration, withdrawal-risk configuration, withdrawal-limit policy configuration, and withdrawal operational review | Required after Building Suit MVP completion and before pilot launch; temporary mini internal queue/reporting UI is allowed only during development; never accessible to regular building or shop users; all actions audited |

No future portal may be treated as a feature bundle inside Building Suit solely to accelerate delivery.

| BUS-RULE-51 | New Owner registration must force Admin review of old owner-related obligations and enforce allowed actions with audit logging before completion. |

| BUS-RULE-53 | Unit finance separates `Monthly / Occupancy Dues`, `Owner Obligations / Special Assessments`, and `Disputed / Under Review`; payments must target exactly one bucket/type. |
| BUS-RULE-54 | Overpayment creates only the credit type approved for the selected bucket/scope: `Occupancy Credit`, `Owner Obligation Credit`, or equalization-derived `Equalization Credit`. |
| BUS-RULE-55 | Building Suit MVP has no cross-building bad-payer score, negative payer score, or reputation system; authorized Admins may review payment history only inside the same Building. |

### 7.2 Subscription Formula

The PRD-approved Phase 1 subscription model is:

```text
monthly_subscription =
  max(active_units_snapshot * EGP 20, EGP 200)
```

The formula uses the canonical active-unit count snapshotted for the billing cycle and begins only after the assigned trial ends. Company-assisted onboarding and self-service creation feed the same membership/active-unit model; neither channel creates a separate billing rule. The EGP 200 minimum applies even where fewer than ten Units are active (BR-OPS-07, BUS-RULE-23/24).

Supporting materials and the pre-interview repository contain conflicting total-unit pricing. Question 11 supersedes that older basis. Sales, forecasts, billing, tests, and customer communication must use active Units plus the minimum fee.

### 7.3 Active Unit Definition

The approved active-unit definition is a non-deleted physical Unit linked to at least one current, approved `active` owner or tenant membership. It is applied consistently to:

- Adoption reporting.
- Governance activation.
- Post-trial subscription billing snapshots.

Binding edge-case treatment:

- A Unit with one active owner, one active tenant, or both is active and is counted once.
- A partially occupied Unit with either an active owner or active tenant is active.
- A vacant Unit is not active.
- Pending, rejected, blocked, `left`, and otherwise ended memberships do not make a Unit active.
- An administrator without a current owner/tenant Unit assignment does not make a Unit active.
- `total_units` remains the declared physical-building size and governance denominator where specified; it is not the subscription multiplier.

### 7.4 Financial Lock Rule

The approved subscription-state behavior is:

- A trial subscription permits approved writes during its assigned zero-charge trial period.
- A `past_due` subscription permits writes during its 14-day grace period.
- After the grace period expires without payment, the subscription transitions to `locked`.
- Locked and cancelled subscriptions reject all building writes.
- Residents see an understandable inactive-subscription state.
- Governance, community, membership, configuration, and finance data remain readable where the visibility policy allows, but cannot be mutated.
- Historical records remain available according to the approved visibility policy.

### 7.5 Governance Threshold Rules

The PRD-approved governance rules are:

- Governance activation: at least 60% active units.
- Normal vote: at least 50% participation.
- Money vote: at least 70% participation.
- Admin replacement: normal-governance quorum of at least 50% participation.
- Vote duration is positive and at most 7 days.
- Participation is calculated against the total-unit snapshot.
- A valid normal or money vote selects the option with the highest count; the threshold is a participation threshold, not a winner-support percentage.
- Admin replacement uses one eligible self-nominated challenger against the incumbent; after normal quorum, majority wins, incumbent majority/tie/no quorum retains the incumbent, and a challenger win transfers the role atomically with no second round. Individual choices are secret; while open ordinary authorized members see participation/progress only and no exact candidate totals, internal tally remains exact/immediate, no voter may change or withdraw their own recorded vote, and no result creates a cooldown.
- The replacement normal result is finalized automatically by the system at the earlier of the scheduled voting deadline or confirmed complete eligible-Unit participation. No business role performs a manual close or result approval.

The PRD resolves activation timing explicitly: manual close, expiry processing, and automatic close all use the creation-time activation, active-unit, and total-unit snapshots. Current occupancy changes never change an open vote's validity or its Unit-based denominator. For an open Admin-replacement cycle they may, however, change which resident is currently eligible to cast an existing Unit's vote, as governed by BUS-RULE-83 and BUS-RULE-84.

## 8. Revenue Model and Financial Planning

### 8.1 Primary Revenue Model

The Phase 1 primary revenue model is a recurring Building subscription based on active Units, with an EGP 200 minimum per billing cycle after trial.

Business rationale:

- Pricing scales with demonstrated resident/owner activation while the minimum protects baseline platform and support cost.
- Small or early-stage Buildings remain bounded by the minimum instead of paying for every vacant/unactivated Unit.
- Founder/company-assisted onboarding supports activation but does not create a parallel or total-unit billing basis.
- The administrator has a direct commercial incentive to invite and retain active residents because activation drives governance value and the billable count.
- Revenue grows with paid Buildings and active Units, and may contract when occupancy/active membership declines.
