# Building Suit Product Requirements Document

## 1. Executive Summary & Product Vision

### 1.1 Product Vision

Building Suit is a mobile-first operational control platform for self-managed residential buildings. It replaces fragmented coordination through messaging applications, paper records, informal cash handling, and spreadsheets with a transparent, unit-centered system for finance, building membership, operational issues, announcements, governance, and local service-provider discovery.

The platform is intended to become a reusable operational infrastructure layer for multiple business domains. Building Suit is the first active portal. Shop Suit, Business Suit, and City Suit are separate future portals connected to a shared identity and platform core.

### 1.2 Core Problem

Self-managed residential buildings commonly operate without a reliable system of record. The supplied documents identify the following recurring problems:

- Monthly charges and collections are recorded manually, making balances difficult to verify.
- Residents lack a trusted view of building income, expenses, and outstanding unit balances.
- Issues, maintenance requests, announcements, and decisions are mixed into unstructured messaging threads.
- Administrative responsibility is concentrated in one person and is difficult to transfer.
- Ownership, tenancy, and unit participation are not modeled consistently.
- Building decisions lack a structured, unit-based voting process.
- Local service providers are discovered through informal referrals without a structured directory.
- Payment operations are manual-first and do not yet have a verified online payment and reconciliation flow.

### 1.3 Primary Value Proposition

Building Suit provides a transparent, auditable, unit-based operating model for residential buildings:

- **For building administrators:** a controlled workflow for residents, charges, payments, expenses, announcements, issues, governance, and subscriptions.
- **For residents:** visibility into unit balances, building financial status, issues, announcements, and building decisions.
- **For owners and tenants:** explicit occupancy modeling with tenant-priority voting where a tenant is assigned.
- **For service providers:** a discoverable service profile that residents can contact externally.
- **For the platform operator:** a hub-and-spoke foundation that supports distinct portals without merging their product interfaces or domain data.

### 1.4 Product Principles

1. **Portal separation is mandatory.** Each portal is an independently deployable frontend application with its own routes, authorization context, state, and domain workflows.
2. **The unit is the operational anchor.** Finance, occupancy, activation, and voting rights are tied to units.
3. **Financial history is auditable.** The ledger is the source of truth, and corrections must use compensating operations rather than destructive edits.
4. **Building-level data is tenant-isolated.** Building-scoped records must always be authorized against the active building context.
5. **Manual-first, gateway-ready.** The MVP supports manually recorded payments and a wallet/manual-balance model while preserving a path to verified gateway processing.
6. **Building funds stay attributable.** Building Suit does not own residents' money or building money. Every building's funds must remain separately attributable, auditable, reconcilable, and backed by an independent balance, ledger, and audit trail regardless of the final Paymob account/subaccount model.
7. **Structured workflows replace chat-based operations.** Issues, announcements, invitations, approvals, and votes must have explicit statuses and permissions.

### 1.5 Product Scope

**MVP and current Building Suit scope**

- Global account and Building Suit portal profile
- Building creation, discovery, selection, and joining
- Units, memberships, owner/tenant assignment, approvals, and blocking
- Charges, manually recorded payments, expenses, ledger, balances, and withdrawal requests
- Building subscription and billing configuration
- Issues, comments, approvals, and closure
- Announcements, urgent announcements, read tracking, and RSVP
- Ordinary normal/money votes and the dedicated Admin replacement cycle
- Notifications and realtime updates
- Permanent manual payment recording for resident payments, even after online collection is activated
- Paymob activation as the top payment-engineering priority, starting from zero

**Explicitly outside the evidenced MVP**

- Service Provider Directory, provider profile creation/editing, provider discovery/contact, provider permissions, provider tables/APIs as active scope, provider booking, in-platform chat with providers, order management, provider payments, and provider moderation
- Implemented Shop Suit, Business Suit, or City Suit product interfaces

## 2. System Architecture & Portal Breakdown

### 2.1 Architectural Model

The platform follows a hub-and-spoke architecture.

- The **hub** owns global users, portal registration, and one profile per user per portal.
- Each **portal** is a separate spoke with independent product routes, domain roles, permissions, UI workflows, and deployable frontend.
- A global user must never belong directly to a building. The supported identity chain is:

```text
auth.users
  -> platform.users
  -> platform.profiles scoped to a portal
  -> building_suit.building_memberships scoped to a building and role
  -> building_suit.units and Building Suit domain data
```

The shared platform core must not become a single product frontend. Shared packages may provide identity clients, API clients, design tokens, validation contracts, and observability utilities, but product navigation and domain state must remain portal-specific.

The target persistence architecture uses one PostgreSQL database per environment with physical schemas organized by bounded domain. Shared global identity and portal registry records belong to `platform`; each portal owns its authoritative records in its own schema, such as `building_suit` or future `shop_suit`. The `public` schema is a migration compatibility layer and must not be the permanent owner of new authoritative domain tables.

The following rules are mandatory across all applications and delivery phases:

- Each portal owns its own pages, route middleware, layouts, feature state, runtime configuration, and deployment pipeline.

- Server-side state management instances must be created per request to prevent cross-user state leakage.
- Protected data must not be serialized into publicly cacheable server-rendered output.
- Cross-portal navigation must be an explicit handoff to another portal deployment, followed by portal-specific profile resolution and authorization.

### 2.4 Routing Strategy

**Building Suit target routes**

```text
/auth/*
/buildings
/buildings/create
/buildings/join
/buildings/:buildingId/*
/finance/*
/community/issues/*
/community/announcements/*
/governance/votes/*
/profile/*
```

The exact route naming may follow Web conventions, but the following middleware sequence is required:

1. Resolve the trusted portal context.
2. Validate or refresh the global authentication session.
3. Resolve the user's profile for the current portal.
4. Resolve the active building where a building-scoped route is requested.
5. Validate active or blocked membership according to the requested operation.
6. Validate role and permission requirements.
7. Validate subscription state for every building write operation.

Shop Suit, Business Suit, City Suit, and Super-Admin must use their own route trees in their own applications. Requires clarification from engineering for their detailed route maps.

### 2.5 State Management Strategy

Each portal manages its own client-side state. The Flutter mobile application uses Riverpod with Drift for durable local data. Nuxt web portals use Pinia for application state and Pinia Colada for server-state queries and mutations.

| State Category | Required Owner | Isolation Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Global session | Shared auth client or portal-local auth store | May be reused across portals only through secure identity handoff; must not contain domain authorization assumptions. |
| Portal profile | Portal-local store | Keyed by portal; exactly one profile per global user per portal. |
| Active building | Building Suit store | Persisted only for Building Suit and validated against current memberships. |
| Permissions | Portal-local authorization store | Derived from current profile, membership, role, and permission assignments. |
| Feature data | Feature-specific stores and query cache | Query keys must include portal and building identifiers where applicable. |
| Realtime updates | Portal-local realtime service | Channels and cache patches must be scoped to the active portal and building. |

### 2.6 Backend and Data Flow

The MVP backend consists of Supabase Auth, PostgreSQL, Row Level Security, Edge Functions, storage, realtime channels, and audit records.

```text
Portal UI
  -> portal-specific API client
  -> Supabase Edge Function
  -> JWT verification
  -> global user and portal-profile resolution
  -> building membership and permission validation
  -> input validation
  -> PostgreSQL read/write and audit record
  -> normalized success or error envelope
  -> query-cache update and/or realtime event
```

Important finance, governance, subscription, payment, and membership writes must not write directly from a client to domain tables. They must pass through an Edge Function or future Node.js command endpoint that resolves the authenticated user and portal profile, validates membership and permissions, executes a PostgreSQL function or transaction, writes an audit record, and returns the standard API response envelope.

The future backend may introduce NestJS with the Fastify adapter, Kysely, PostgREST, Redis, BullMQ, and additional realtime infrastructure incrementally. It must continue using the same PostgreSQL source of truth, migrations, authorization model, audit records, and API contracts.

Current API behavior:

- Protected requests provide a bearer token.
- Backend authorization resolves the current Building Suit profile and relevant building membership.
- Public UUIDs are resolved to internal identifiers server-side.
- Writes are performed through privileged server clients after explicit authorization checks.
- Responses use structured error codes and status codes.
- Successful JSON responses use `Cache-Control: no-store`.

Target multi-portal backend requirements:

- Authentication helpers must resolve the current portal from trusted server context rather than hardcoding `building_suit`.
- Every portal-domain record must carry a portal or domain-tenant scope where required.
- Shared global records must be owned by `platform`; Building Suit records must be owned by `building_suit`; future portal records must be owned by their corresponding portal schemas.
- Cross-portal reads and writes must use approved contracts, views, or commands rather than direct coupling between portal-owned tables.
- `public` must remain a temporary compatibility layer and must not receive new authoritative domain tables.
- Shared Edge Functions must verify the portal profile before resolving domain permissions.
- Service-role credentials must remain server-side only.

### 2.7 Tenant and Data Isolation

- Building Suit data is scoped by `building_id`.
- Portal identity is scoped by `profile.portal_id`.
- Physical schema placement provides bounded-domain ownership but does not replace RLS, grants, or tenant checks.
- Reads and writes must enforce portal, profile, building, membership, and permission constraints.
- Blocked building members may retain read-only access where explicitly supported but must not perform building write operations.
- Units, votes, ledger records, wallets, transactions, and audit records must not be destructively deleted.
- Exact deletion and retention behavior for user profiles, future provider profiles if reintroduced, files, and notifications requires clarification from engineering.

## 3. Core Features & Business Logic

### 3.1 Identity and Portal Profiles

**Required behavior**

- A global authenticated user maps to an application-owned `platform.users` record.
- The user must authenticate once through the shared identity layer.
- Building Suit MVP authentication includes Egyptian-phone/password and Google OAuth. Email/password login is not part of this checkpoint.
- Login accepts the verified Egyptian phone number linked to the Account, and Google remains available through the approved Google flow. Email login is not part of this checkpoint and requires a future explicit decision.
- Registration always displays both credential fields. A valid unique Egyptian phone number is required, while email is visibly labeled optional. No Email / Phone / Both method selector is shown.
- The required phone must be verified before normal navigation. Email is optional, may be added later from Profile or Account Settings, may replace/update an existing email through a secure verification flow, supports request/resend verification, shows verified/unverified state, and must not block app access, first login, Home access, building access, or core product usage when missing or unverified.
- Email verification is required only before email-based notifications or messages are sent.
- Registration has no email-based OTP step. Phone verification uses a six-digit WhatsApp OTP.
- Password recovery uses the verified Egyptian phone path for MVP. Email recovery is not part of this checkpoint and requires a future explicit decision.
- Phone numbers must be valid Egyptian mobile numbers normalized to the canonical `+20` format.
- Successful first time Google authentication callback routes to the **One Last Step** screen. That screen is used only for first time Google authentication and completes any outstanding required phone verification and Full Name field before normal Building Suit navigation.
- A Google login attempt must not silently create a new Account when the user has not chosen registration.
- Fingerprint sign-in is an optional device-local convenience and is never available for the first login. After one successful password or Google login, the user may enable or disable fingerprint sign-in at any time from Security settings on a supported device.
- Enabling fingerprint sign-in requires an authenticated session and successful operating-system biometric confirmation. Failure, cancellation, unsupported hardware, or invalidated device biometrics falls back to normal password or Google login without weakening server authentication.
- An authenticated Account without a Building membership may continue into a restricted exploration experience. Exploration grants no Building-scoped access and keeps Create Building and Join Building available.
- A user has one profile per portal.
- On entry to a portal, the system must resolve or provision the user's profile for that portal.
- Portal permissions must be derived only from the current portal profile and its domain assignments.
- Portal roles are not stored directly on the global user.
- A global session must not grant cross-portal domain access by itself.

### 3.2 Building Creation and Activation

**Onboarding channels**

A building reaches Building Suit through exactly one of two channels:

1. **Self-service** — an authenticated user creates the building directly (flow below).
2. **Company-assisted** — the same user instead submits an onboarding request and a Platform Operator completes creation on their behalf, performing first activation (PRD §3.2.1).

Both channels produce the same building record, the same subscription behavior, and the same administrator outcome. Neither channel is required; the user chooses.

**Location-first discovery**

1. Before naming a building, the user supplies a location, either by current-location detection or by selecting a point on a map.
2. The system returns buildings already created within approximately five miles of that location so the user can recognize an existing building instead of creating a duplicate.
3. From the discovery result, the user either requests to join a listed building (PRD §3.3; UX §1.6), continues to self-service creation below, or submits a company-assisted onboarding request (PRD §3.2.1).

**Self-service building-creation flow**

4. The selected location produces an auto-filled address. The address remains editable, and the user may add an optional landmark or distinguishing note, such as "next to a mosque", "in front of a school", or "beside a market".
5. The initial building name is generated from the location-derived address and remains editable before final creation.
6. The user submits the editable building name and total-unit count in addition to the location already captured.
7. The name must contain 2 to 100 characters.
8. Total units must be a positive integer no greater than 1,000.
9. Latitude and longitude must be valid.
10. The backend re-checks for nearby buildings within approximately five miles at final submission, since the location may have been refined since the initial check.
11. If a possible duplicate is found, the API returns a `409` proximity warning unless the user explicitly continues.
12. The backend creates the building, units, administrator membership, subscription record, and optional creator unit assignment.
13. The building is active from the moment of creation, and the requesting user becomes its administrator.

**Activation distinction**

- Building operational status is active from creation. Missing billing configuration does not change that status and does not block join requests; it prevents only finance operations that require a billing configuration.
- Governance activation is computed separately as:

```text
governance_activation_rate = active_units / total_units * 100
governance_is_activated = governance_activation_rate >= 60
```

- Governance activation and the active-unit definition are unchanged by the onboarding channel chosen and by the subscription formula in PRD §3.6. Both continue to depend only on real, approved owner/tenant memberships.

### 3.2.1 Company-Assisted Onboarding and First Activation

- An authenticated user may submit a company-assisted onboarding request from the location-first discovery step instead of creating a building directly. The request captures the requester's contact details, the chosen location, and any known building name or unit count.
- During pilot, the founder personally owns company-assisted onboarding fulfillment. A temporary restricted internal queue may support development, but the full Super-Admin portal must own the production claim/fulfillment workflow before pilot launch.
- Published pilot SLA: an onboarding request is claimed within 1 business day and fulfilled within 5 business days, subject to requester responsiveness and complete required information.
- The claiming Platform Operator performs the building creation on the requester's behalf. First activation means the operator records verified owner/tenant occupancy for the building's units directly, from the roster supplied by the requester, instead of waiting for each resident to self-register and be approved individually.
- First activation is an operational onboarding service, not a separate governance or billing mechanism. It records ordinary memberships that feed the same canonical active-unit projection used by self-service Buildings. Billing still begins only after the assigned trial; its amount follows the approved active-unit formula regardless of onboarding channel (PRD §3.6).
- Once the Platform Operator marks the request fulfilled, the requester receives administrator access to the created building and the request closes.
- A pending request may be cancelled by the requester or rejected by a Platform Operator; rejection requires a reason and does not block the requester from creating the building themselves afterward.
- Company-assisted onboarding is an additional channel. The self-service flow above remains available at all times.

### 3.3 Units, Memberships, Owners, and Tenants

The system models membership separately from unit occupancy:

- A membership binds a profile, building, role, status, and optional unit.
- A unit may have no more than one current owner and one current tenant.
- Owner and tenant are occupancy roles used for unit assignment and voting priority.
- Administrator and resident are portal authorization roles in the current shared constants and permission model.
- A user may hold different roles in different buildings.

**Join flow**

1. A user identifies a building by public identifier or join code.
2. The user selects a unit and requests owner or tenant occupancy.
3. Duplicate requests and incompatible occupied-unit assignments are rejected.
4. An administrator approves or rejects the request.
5. Where replacement is needed, the operation must require an explicit swap/replace decision.

**Membership statuses**

- `active`: may read and perform actions allowed by permissions.
- `blocked`: may retain explicitly supported read-only access but may not write.
- `left` or equivalent ended status: no active building access; occupancy history is ended.

**Canonical active-unit definition**

- An active unit is a non-deleted physical unit with at least one current, approved `active` membership assigned to it as owner or tenant.
- A unit with only `blocked`, pending, rejected, `left`, or otherwise ended memberships is not active.
- A vacant unit with no current active owner or tenant is not active.
- Either one active owner or one active tenant is sufficient; a partially occupied unit is active and is counted once.
- An administrator membership without a current owner/tenant unit assignment does not make a unit active.
- `unit.is_active` is a derived/projection value of this rule and must not be treated as an independently editable business decision.
- The same canonical count drives governance activation, reporting, customer-facing activation explanations, and the post-trial subscription formula. Subscription billing snapshots the approved active-unit count for each billing cycle (PRD §3.6).

**Required history rule**

- Unit and occupancy history must be retained indefinitely and must not be destructively deleted.
- A user must have access to their complete residential history, including all past units they were assigned to across all buildings.
- A unit must retain its full financial and residential history. Past residents can view only the portion of that history that falls within their own occupancy period. Administrators can view the full unit history at all times.
- **Occupancy-period balance behavior:** Unit balances are scoped to the resident/occupancy period that created them:
  - When a resident leaves, the system warns the administrator if the old resident has remaining Occupancy Credit or debt, but does not block the exit and does not block assigning a new resident.
  - The new resident starts from zero and does not inherit the old resident's credit or debt.
  - Old credit or debt remains preserved historically against the old resident and old occupancy period.
  - No automatic settlement expense, refund, or debt transfer is created on exit.
  - If the administrator pays money back to the old resident outside the app, the administrator may record a building expense of type `Unit Settlement Expense`.
  - If an old resident pays after leaving, the administrator records it as `Past Resident Payment` / `Late Settlement`; it links to the old occupancy period, reduces only that historical debt, and does not change the current resident's opening balance.
- **Owner-obligation history behavior:** Owner-related obligations are period-aware and must not be silently transferred to a new Owner:
  - `Old Owner Debt` remains tied to the known previous Owner and previous ownership period, may be visible to Admin in unit history, and does not affect the new Owner opening balance.
  - `Unassigned Owner Responsibility` remains tied to the Unit while there is no registered Owner or ownership is unclear; when a new Owner is registered, Admin may assign it to the new Owner, leave it unassigned, or waive it with a clear reason.
  - `Pre-First-Owner Unit Obligation` is tied to the Unit before first handover, defaults to the first Owner, and may be waived only with a clear reason and audit log.
  - If an Admin assigns `Unassigned Owner Responsibility` or `Pre-First-Owner Unit Obligation` to a new Owner, the item requires in-app acknowledgment or documented external acknowledgment before it becomes a payable due.
  - If the Owner disputes the assigned old obligation, it becomes `Disputed / Under Review`, does not become payable, does not block Owner registration or normal app use, and remains visible to Admin until resolved by documented cancellation, waiver, confirmation with evidence, or external acknowledgment.
- A user assigned to multiple units simultaneously follows the same history rules for each unit independently.

### 3.4 Authorization and Permission Evaluation

The required authorization chain is:

```text
global user
  -> current portal profile
  -> building membership
  -> role
  -> role permissions
  -> requested operation
```

Authorization behavior:

- Active membership is required for writes.
- Blocked membership may be accepted for explicitly read-only endpoints.
- `locked` and `cancelled` subscriptions make the building read-only: users may sign in and view approved historical data, but all building writes are disabled.
- Trial, active, and past-due subscriptions within grace permit writes subject to role, permission, and operation-specific policy.
- Voting requires an eligible active unit in addition to `vote.cast`.
- Administrative operations require explicit permissions rather than UI visibility alone.

### 3.5 Finance and Ledger

#### 3.5.1 Ledger Model

The ledger is the financial source of truth. Financial records must be traceable to the building and, where applicable, unit and profile.

Account types:

| Account Type | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `receivable` | Tracks amounts owed by units and payments credited against those amounts. |
| `wallet` | Tracks funds represented as held within the platform or gateway-facing wallet model. The final Paymob model is not decided and may become per-building Paymob subaccounts/wallets or a single platform account/wallet with internal per-building separation after onboarding, KYC, contracting, legal/compliance review, technical validation, and payout/reconciliation testing. |
| `gateway_pending` | Tracks trusted Paymob resident collections that are confirmed and counted as building collections but not yet available for withdrawal/use until Paymob settlement availability is confirmed. |
| `manual` | Tracks funds handled outside the platform, such as cash, Instapay or other manually recorded payments. |
| `expense` | Tracks building expenses. |

The binding operational sign convention is:

```text
net(account scope) = debits - credits
unit_receivable_balance = receivable_debits - receivable_credits
total_receivable = receivable_debits - receivable_credits
total_paid = receivable_credits
total_expenses = expense_debits
wallet_balance = wallet_debits - wallet_credits
manual_balance = manual_debits - manual_credits
settlement_pending_balance = gateway_pending_debits - gateway_pending_credits
total_building_balance = wallet_balance + manual_balance + settlement_pending_balance
withdrawal_reserved_amount = sum(Pending and Requires Review withdrawal reservations)
available_balance = wallet_balance + manual_balance - withdrawal_reserved_amount
```

For `receivable`, positive net means amount due and negative net means unit credit. For `manual` and `wallet`, positive net means funds available. This convention supersedes every earlier or downstream `credits - debits` wallet formula.

The balance experience and read models must distinguish total building balance, collected but not yet available for withdrawal, amount reserved/held for pending withdrawals, available balance for withdrawal/use, completed withdrawals, and failed or reversed withdrawals. A `Pending` or `Requires Review` withdrawal is a held/reserved amount, not a completed expense or outflow in final financial totals. A new withdrawal request may use only available balance after excluding settlement-pending collections and active withdrawal reservations.

The withdrawal-request code currently calculates wallet availability. The final payout, wallet, custody, settlement, and licensing model must be validated with Paymob, legal/compliance, finance operations, and engineering before activation. Building Suit must not assume that any regulatory, licensing, custody, or payment-service requirement is absent.

#### 3.5.2 Charges

- An administrator with `finance.record` may create a positive charge for a unit.
- A charge creates a receivable debit.
- A charge may include a due date and description.
- Monthly maintenance/occupancy charge generation runs automatically at the beginning of each month according to the active Billing Config and may also be triggered manually by an Admin for the first operating month, retry after technical failure, generation after configuration setup, or operational recovery without Platform Support.
- Monthly maintenance/occupancy dues apply only to Units with a current active resident, current active occupancy, or eligible active profile according to the approved occupancy rules. They do not apply to every physical Unit by default.
- Monthly maintenance/occupancy dues are scoped to the current occupancy period: old resident debt remains historical to the old resident/occupancy period, the new resident starts from zero, and ordinary monthly maintenance does not become permanent Unit debt that automatically transfers to a new resident.
- The due day is clamped to a maximum of day 28 during monthly generation.
- Monthly generation does not currently prorate partial months.
- Duplicate monthly charges are prevented per building, unit, configuration, and month.
- `Special Assessment with Deferred Equalization` is a separate financial concept for exceptional shared building improvements such as cameras, elevator work, intercom, building gate, facade painting, or major shared infrastructure. It is not normal monthly maintenance.
- A Special Assessment has three explicit stages. Draft/preview stores only proposed project, Unit, funder, responsibility, fair-share, Deferred Equalization, due-date, and document data; it creates no payable obligation, debt, ledger entry, Equalization Credit, or Building-balance movement. Its linked Money Vote must meet the approved 70% participation threshold; failed, expired, no-quorum, or cancelled outcomes have zero financial effect, and every material revision invalidates approval and requires a new Money Vote. Only API-088 activation after a successful unchanged linked vote freezes the immutable distribution/original-funder snapshot and creates obligations and ledger effects exactly once under retry/concurrency.
- Example: for a 10,000 EGP camera project across 20 total Units with 10 active funders, each active funder initially pays 1,000 EGP, the fair share is 500 EGP per Unit, and each inactive/no-owner Unit receives a 500 EGP deferred pending obligation. When one delayed Unit later pays 500 EGP, only the 10 original funders receive 50 EGP each as `Equalization Credit`. Later payers do not join the original-funder redistribution group.
- For Special Assessments tied to building/unit assets, the Owner is the primary responsible party. If the Owner is unavailable or not registered, the current resident may pay on behalf of the Owner, but the record must distinguish responsible party, actual payer, linked Unit, and linked ownership/occupancy period where applicable.
- If no Owner is registered for an owner-responsibility Special Assessment, create `Owner Responsibility Pending` on the Unit. Do not automatically convert it to resident debt.

#### 3.5.3 Payments

- A Building Administrator records a positive payment against a building and unit. Manual payment recording remains permanently available after Paymob goes live; it is not only an outage fallback.
- Platform Operators may support manual-payment operations only where future privileged support access explicitly permits. Regular residents must not directly record authoritative manual payments.
- Supported method values are `cash`, `instapay`, `wallet`, and `other`.
- Every payment must be directed to one specific due bucket/type. A generic payment that automatically mixes buckets is not allowed.
- Approved due buckets are `Monthly / Occupancy Dues`, `Owner Obligations / Special Assessments`, and `Disputed / Under Review`.
- Monthly payments create a receivable credit for the selected current occupancy period and may create `Occupancy Credit` if the amount exceeds the selected bucket due. Occupancy Credit is tied to the current resident/occupancy period, applies to future Monthly / Occupancy Dues for that same period, and does not transfer automatically to a new resident.
- Owner-obligation or Special Assessment payments create a receivable credit for the selected owner/unit scope and may create `Owner Obligation Credit` if the amount exceeds the selected bucket due. Owner Obligation Credit is tied to the Owner, ownership period, and Unit, applies to future Owner Obligations or Special Assessments for that same owner/unit scope, and does not transfer automatically to a new Owner unless later explicitly approved.
- `Equalization Credit` is created from delayed Special Assessment payments for entitled original funders only. By default it is a future discount for the entitled person/ownership/occupancy period and may reduce future dues/payments according to the payment-bucket rules.
- A credit can be either used as a future discount or paid out physically through `Equalization Payout` / `Special Assessment Settlement`; the same amount can never be used twice.
- If the person entitled to Equalization Credit leaves before using it, the credit remains tied to that person and old occupancy/ownership period. It does not automatically transfer to the new resident or new Owner; Admin may preserve it historically or settle it through payout.
- `Disputed / Under Review` items are not payable by default. They can be paid only after resolution into a payable due, or through a separately approved documented pay-under-dispute flow. Generic payments and credits must not silently apply to disputed items.
- **No cross-building credit score:** MVP has no shared bad-payer score, negative payer score visible to other buildings, I-Score-style credit concept, or cross-building reputation system. Payment history is visible only inside the same Building as historical information for authorized Admin review.
- A `cash` or `other` payment also creates a manual debit, increasing recorded manual funds.
- A `wallet` payment instead creates a wallet credit, deducting the same amount from available wallet funds; it must not also create a manual debit.
- Payments operate on a ledger basis within the selected bucket/scope. They are not silently allocated across unrelated due types.
- Before payment confirmation, the UI/API contract must show selected due bucket, current due in that bucket, applicable credits/discounts, amount being paid, excess amount if any, new credit created by overpayment, remaining due after payment, whether any credit is applied, and whether any credit is excluded because it belongs to another bucket/scope.
- If a Paymob resident-payment fee exists, the resident pays it on top of the due/payment amount. The fee must be shown before payment, Building Suit must not assume fee existence or value before Paymob validation, and if the actual fee is zero the receipt shows fee `0`.
- Resident Paymob payment fees are not building funds. They appear in the resident receipt/payment history and reconciliation data, but do not enter building balance, do not reduce the unit balance, and are not recorded as building expenses.
- After trusted Paymob confirmation, the resident/unit debt is reduced immediately and the payment is shown as confirmed/collected, but the amount remains `Collected but not yet available for withdrawal` until Paymob confirms settlement or withdrawal availability.
- If a Paymob-confirmed payment is later reversed, refunded, or charged back, the original payment remains immutable evidence. A compensating ledger operation restores the selected bucket amount due or reduces Occupancy Credit / Owner Obligation Credit according to the reversal amount and bucket scope, updates building balances according to the final Paymob result, and shows the reversal/refund/chargeback in payment history and the financial timeline.
- The MVP has no direct refund button for Building Administrators or Residents. Refund cases are handled only by Platform Support / Operations after Paymob review and evidence validation, and the final result is recorded in the ledger without deleting or editing the original payment.

#### 3.5.4 Expenses

- An administrator records a positive expense with a required title.
- The operation creates an expense debit and a manual-balance credit.
- Expenses reduce the manual balance.
- Expense creation must be auditable.
- Outside the separate Emergency Expense workflow, an ordinary expense may be posted without Money Vote authorization only when the server-authoritative cumulative amount for its underlying spending purpose/project does not exceed the Building's effective no-vote spending limit. Splitting one purpose across rows never resets that limit.
- Every ordinary expense belongs to a first-class spending purpose/project. The server aggregates same-purpose operations transactionally and uses Building, purpose, vendor/payee, category, invoice/order/contract reference, normalized description similarity, evidence linkage, time proximity, and suspicious fragmentation as auditable anti-circumvention signals. Exact fuzzy-detection thresholds remain an Engineering/Security design item; explicit same-purpose aggregation is deterministic.
- When an expense uses Money Vote authorization, linking the relevant successful Money Vote is mandatory and server-validated, not descriptive metadata. One authorization may fund multiple expenses/installments for the same approved purpose, but their cumulative effective posted amount may never exceed the winning option's base amount plus its approved tolerance.
- A successful generic Money Vote is authorization only. It does not debit or reserve Building funds, create an Expense or payable obligation, post to the ledger, or prove that money was spent. Actual financial operations are separate linked commands. The dedicated Special Assessment activation boundary remains unchanged.
- `Unit Settlement Expense` records a real payout made outside the app to settle an old resident's historical Occupancy Credit. It must not affect a new resident's opening balance.
- `Equalization Payout` / `Special Assessment Settlement` records a physical payout of Equalization Credit to the entitled person. It reduces building balance, links to the person, role, Unit, and original Special Assessment, marks the corresponding Equalization Credit settled/paid out, and prevents later reuse of the same credit as a discount.

#### 3.5.5 Withdrawals

- The current Building Administrator can request a withdrawal directly from the building's available funds to a bank account or e-wallet destination. Available funds exclude Paymob collections that are confirmed but not yet settlement-available and exclude active `Pending` / `Requires Review` withdrawal reservations. Routine Platform Operator or Super-Admin approval is not required for a normal withdrawal request.
- Building Suit must not store a building-owned or Admin-owned beneficiary list, must not transfer payout destinations from an old Admin to a new Admin, and must not depend on stored beneficiary data before a withdrawal can be created.
- For each withdrawal, the current Building Administrator may enter a new bank-account or e-wallet destination, or select a destination saved by the device, browser, or Paymob experience only if that capability is supported. The final Paymob-provided destination-saving UX is capability-gated and must not be finalized before Paymob onboarding, contract scope confirmation, technical validation, and payout-flow testing.
- If Paymob, a bank, or another provider charges a withdrawal/payout fee, the building bears the fee. The fee must be shown before withdrawal confirmation, the reserved amount must equal withdrawal amount plus fee, and the fee must be recorded transparently in the ledger as part of the withdrawal operation. Building Suit must not assume whether fees exist or what their amount is before Paymob contract/product validation; if the actual fee is zero, show fee `0`.
- Any withdrawal method that cannot show the final fee and total reserved amount before request creation must not be enabled in the MVP. Estimated withdrawal fees, hidden post-execution fees, and payout methods without pre-confirmed final fees are not allowed.
- Building Suit retains only minimum necessary withdrawal evidence: amount, destination type, masked destination data when available, Paymob transaction/transfer reference, requesting Admin, timestamps, current and historical state, and callback/reconciliation evidence. Full sensitive payout destination details must not be stored or displayed unless a later legal, contractual, or Paymob technical requirement makes a minimum data element mandatory.
- A Super-Admin-configured withdrawal threshold requires step-up authentication at or above the configured amount. Step-up is a re-login-like confirmation using the Admin's password or already-enabled supported device biometrics; the Admin does not re-enter a phone number and no OTP is used. Successful and failed step-up attempts must be auditable.
- Super-Admin controls withdrawal risk configuration and daily withdrawal limits through restricted internal operations only. This includes the global daily-limit enabled/disabled state, global daily-limit amount when enabled, step-up withdrawal threshold, pending-to-review duration, size/category-based policies, and building-specific overrides. The most specific valid policy applies in this precedence: building-specific, building-size/category, global default, then no daily limit when the global policy is disabled and no more-specific rule applies. Policy and configuration changes must audit actor, prior value, new value, effective time, scope, and audit linkage. Building Admins and Residents must never read or override this policy surface.
- Daily-limit calculations use `Africa/Cairo`, reset at `00:00:00`, and end at `23:59:59`; rolling 24-hour windows are not used. Each withdrawal persists an immutable `withdrawal_limit_day` at creation and remains attached to that day even if callbacks arrive after midnight.
- When a withdrawal is sent to Paymob, the amount is immediately reserved from both available building balance and the available daily limit for its `withdrawal_limit_day`. The reservation remains while the request is `Pending` or `Requires Review`, remains consumed on `Confirmed`, and is released back to the original day on `Failed` or `Reversed`.
- The backend updates withdrawal state only from trusted Paymob callbacks/webhooks or documented reconciliation evidence. Browser return state, client-side success screens, local timeout, and closed UI state are never sources of truth.
- After submission, the Building Administrator cannot cancel the withdrawal. There must be no Admin-facing cancellation action after creation.
- If Paymob does not provide a final callback within a Super-Admin-configured duration, the withdrawal moves from `Pending` to `Requires Review`. Reservations remain in place and the item appears in a Super-Admin operational review queue.
- After documented Paymob review or reconciliation, a Platform Operator / Super-Admin may record a final primary status of `Confirmed`, `Failed`, or `Reversed`, or record a reconciliation review outcome of `Still Pending`. `Still Pending` is not a primary withdrawal status: the primary status remains `Requires Review`, balance and daily-limit reservations remain held, and the event records reviewer, timestamp, evidence/reference where appropriate, and safe reason/note. This is not discretionary manual confirmation: every final status update requires Paymob reference or reconciliation evidence.
- After a documented `Failed` or `Reversed` result, the original withdrawal remains immutable historical evidence, reserved balance and daily-limit reservation are released, and the Building Administrator may create a new independent withdrawal that applies the active balance, limit, destination, confirmation, and step-up rules again.
- Authorized residents see a withdrawal immediately after creation, including while `Pending`. The compact ledger/table row shows one primary withdrawal record with amount, requesting Admin, current status, destination type, masked destination when available, and creation time; opening the record shows a resident-safe timeline/history. Residents must not see full destination data, raw Paymob callback bodies, callback event IDs, gateway secrets, internal reconciliation evidence, private operator notes, or unnecessary beneficiary data. State changes must not create duplicate ledger rows for the same withdrawal.

Required production state model:

```text
Pending
  -> Confirmed
  -> Failed
  -> Reversed
  -> Requires Review

Requires Review
  -> Confirmed
  -> Failed
  -> Reversed
  -> reconciliation review outcome: Still Pending
     - primary status remains Requires Review
     - balance reservation remains held
     - daily-limit reservation remains held
```

All primary status transitions and review-outcome events must be idempotent and audited. `Pending` and `Requires Review` are held/reserved states, not completed outflows. `Still Pending` never releases reservations and never posts a final outflow.

### 3.6 Subscription and Billing

Subscriptions control whole-building write availability after the grace period and cancellation.

**Required behavior**

- All building writes are rejected when a subscription is locked or cancelled, including finance writes, membership changes, issues, comments, announcements, votes, governance actions, building configuration, and subscription-affecting building operations.
- Users may still sign in and view approved historical data while locked or cancelled.
- A `past_due` subscription enters a grace period during which writes remain permitted. After the grace period expires without payment, the subscription transitions to `locked` and the building becomes read-only.
- The grace period duration is 14 days from the date the subscription becomes `past_due`.
- The default trial is 90 days from building creation and has zero subscription charge. No subscription charge accrues during the trial.
- Billing begins only after the assigned trial ends. After trial, the monthly charge is `MAX(active_units × 20 EGP, 200 EGP)` using the canonical active-unit count snapshotted for the billing cycle.
- Trial duration is configurable from Super-Admin through versioned trial policies. A policy change applies only to buildings created on or after the policy effective date; existing buildings keep the trial duration assigned at creation.
- The trial countdown is visible to Building Administrators only and shows remaining trial days and first billing date.
- Future subscription price changes may apply to all buildings, including existing buildings, but only after a clear notice period before the effective date.
- Every affected active Building Suit profile must receive both an in-app notice and an external notice through the company-configured external delivery channel(s). Building Admin dashboard/banner and subscription-page displays may additionally show the new price and effective date, but the recipient requirement is not Admin-only.
- Super-Admin pricing-policy configuration controls templates, timing, applicability, and configured external delivery channel(s); the exact external provider may remain configurable or activation-gated.
- Every pricing-policy change requires an audit log, creation date, effective date, notice period, applicability rule, audit actor, and delivery evidence.
- If a Building Administrator cancels the subscription, the building moves to read-only for a 3-month retention period. During that period, subscription can be reactivated without data loss; after that period, retention/deletion follows the approved PDPL/privacy/retention policy.
- Cancellation is blocked while the building wallet has a positive balance. The administrator must withdraw the full wallet balance first, and the UI must show a clear blocking message. Existing negative balance responsibility transfers to the successor administrator; if no successor exists, resolution must use a Platform Operator / Super-Admin process once defined.

### 3.7 Governance and Voting

#### 3.7.1 Vote Types and Thresholds

| Vote Type | Participation Threshold | Approval Before Opening | Additional Rules |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Normal | 50% of total-unit snapshot | Resident-created votes require admin approval; admin-created votes open directly. | At least two options. |
| Money | 70% of total-unit snapshot | Resident-created votes require admin approval; admin-created votes open directly. | The server-owned topic is `spending_authorization` or `no_vote_limit_change`, never inferred from text. Spending authorization uses one or more positive-amount spend options plus exactly one explicit no-spend option, with tolerance/exposure/validity visible before voting. A limit-change ballot instead contains one proposed limit and exactly the server-derived `approve_proposed_limit` and `keep_current_limit` choices; spending-authorization fields do not apply. |
| Admin replacement | 50% normal-governance quorum | Never requires current-Admin approval. | Exactly the incumbent and one eligible self-nominated challenger; the normal nonterminated schedule is a fixed 24-hour announcement followed by a three-day ballot whose scheduled deadline is `voting_end_at`. The normal result is finalized automatically by the server at the earlier of `voting_end_at` or confirmed complete eligible-Unit participation; no person closes, approves, or delays it. Challenger withdrawal during announcement or voting, or trusted challenger-ineligibility termination while nonterminal, may end the cycle earlier with no winner or Admin transfer; the incumbent remains and no outcome creates a cooldown. One cycle at a time. |

All vote types currently require a governance activation rate of at least 60%.

#### 3.7.2 Vote Creation Rules

- Title is required for ordinary Normal and Money votes only.
- Ordinary vote type must be `normal` or `money`; Admin replacement uses its dedicated cycle APIs/state machine.
- An Admin-replacement cycle has no resident-authored title. Its identity is the incumbent Admin versus the self-nominated challenger, and any displayed label or summary is generated by the system from those two identities. Self-nomination must not accept, require, or store a user-entered election title.
- Duration must be positive and no more than 7 days.
- Normal votes require at least two options. Every Money Vote carries the stable server-owned `money_vote_topic = spending_authorization|no_vote_limit_change`; title, description, labels, category, and amount never determine the topic.
- A `spending_authorization` Money Vote uses either one structured spend option plus exactly one explicit no-spend option, or multiple structured spend options plus that option. Every spend option has a positive amount; the no-spend option has no positive amount. Tolerance defaults to 0%, may be explicitly selected only up to the versioned Money Vote-constraint maximum, and is immutable after voting starts. The winning option's base amount, tolerance percentage, calculated maximum authorized amount, purpose, and execution-validity duration must be shown before voting.
- A `no_vote_limit_change` Money Vote accepts one `proposed_no_vote_limit`. The server resolves and freezes the current effective limit/provenance/Building policy version and applicable platform maximum/version, then derives exactly `approve_proposed_limit` and `keep_current_limit`. Client-authored options or labels, arbitrary option amounts, no-spend, financial mode, tolerance, execution validity, purpose linkage, and calculated spending exposure are forbidden. The proposed value must satisfy the approved EGP string-decimal/financial-value contract, differ from the current limit, and not exceed the snapshotted maximum. At most one such ballot may be open per Building.
- Admin replacement cannot be created as a generic vote. A voting-eligible active Owner/resident other than the incumbent may self-nominate only; nobody may nominate another person. The confirmed self-nomination creates the dedicated incumbent-versus-one-challenger cycle directly, without nominee acceptance, current-Admin approval, or Platform approval.
- Total units, active units, and activation rate are snapshotted at creation.

#### 3.7.2A Ordinary Vote Request Review Lifecycle

- Resident-created ordinary vote requests are Building-scoped requests, not Admin-owned work items. They remain tied to the Building when the Building Admin changes.
- Request status is `pending_approval`, `overdue`, `approved`, `rejected`, or `withdrawn`. `approved` opens the ordinary vote; `rejected` retains the request, versions, rejection reason, and audit trail; `withdrawn` retains the request, versions, withdrawn state, withdrawing author, withdrawal time, and audit trail.
- Only the request author may edit resident-submitted content. Editing is allowed before approval, including the same rejected or withdrawn request before resubmission. After Admin approval, the approved content is permanently non-editable by every party.
- The Building Admin must never edit, rewrite, or replace resident-submitted content. Admin authority is limited to approval, rejection with a documented reason, and the approved-vote cancellation rule below.
- A request author may withdraw an undecided request at any time before Admin approval or rejection, including while it is overdue. Withdrawal preserves the request and all versions; it does not hard-delete anything.
- Rejected and withdrawn requests may be edited and resubmitted as the same request. Every rejected or withdrawn version, rejection reason, and prior state remains immutable history. A rejected-request resubmission receives a full new Admin-review window calculated from the resubmission timestamp; no prior elapsed or remaining time carries forward. A withdrawn request has no product-imposed resubmission deadline: while the author's same original Building membership occurrence remains active and the author currently satisfies all submission eligibility rules, the author may review, edit, and resubmit it at any later time, and a successful withdrawn-request resubmission likewise receives a full new Admin-review window calculated from the resubmission timestamp (Section 6.8I; retention alone never guarantees current edit/resubmit eligibility).
- While withdrawn, the request is outside ordinary Building Admin surfaces. Temporary ineligibility removes edit/resubmit until eligibility returns. Permanent original-membership end removes edit/resubmit forever, including after rejoin, but the active authenticated original author retains the narrow own-account history read and applicable Hide/Restore personal preference without any current membership or Building authority.
- Hide is available only on the original author's own visible record whose canonical current state is exactly `withdrawn`; Restore only on its own hidden record while still `withdrawn`. Every non-withdrawn state exposes neither. These presentation-only operations remain available during temporary ineligibility and after membership end, never delete/mutate the record or evidence, and are rechecked under the locked preference transaction. Resubmission-first rejects stale Hide; Hide-first is cleared atomically by successful resubmission, so no active request remains hidden. No prerequisite Restore or hard-delete right exists.
- Resubmission of a withdrawn request uses the ordinary final Submit action after the normal review/edit experience — no separate confirmation, acknowledgement, or one-tap list resubmission — and revalidates all current server-authoritative product, membership, eligibility, data, voting, and request rules at resubmission time; the original submission's validity is not grandfathered. Invalid content is rejected with safe actionable field/global errors, the request stays withdrawn, and the author updates the same request. After a successful resubmission, the Building Admin's normal review surface shows only the current submitted content version plus a neutral lifecycle timeline (submitted, withdrawn, resubmitted, with authoritative event times); prior content versions remain immutable restricted audit evidence and never appear in ordinary Admin review. The request enters the review queue by its latest successful submission time with no special priority or penalty, and the current Building Admin receives the ordinary immediate in-app review notification with neutral resubmission context.
- Withdrawal, resubmission, approval, and rejection are concurrency-safe server-authoritative state transitions: the first valid transition that commits atomically establishes the final state, a committed withdrawal immediately invalidates Building Admin approval/rejection for that request version, a committed decision likewise rejects a competing withdrawal, and stale commands receive a stable safe conflict response. Client clocks, button-press order, and last-write-wins behavior never decide the winner.
- While `pending_approval` or `overdue`, request content is visible only to the request author and the current Building Admin. Rejected request content, versions, and rejection reasons are visible only to the request author and current Building Admin. Other residents do not see pending or rejected content.
- The request author alone retains product-facing access to their withdrawn request, versions, state, and history so they can edit and resubmit that same request. After a pre-decision withdrawal, the current Building Admin loses content/history visibility and every other resident remains denied. The record and audit history are retained internally and never hard-deleted, but internal privileged audit/security retention is not Admin-facing visibility and does not create a broader Super-Admin/Operator product audience.
- The default Admin-review duration is 48 hours. Only the company-level Super Admin may change the global platform-wide value in the Building Suit platform administration area. Building Admins cannot configure or override it. Changes apply only to new vote requests submitted after the change; existing requests retain the review deadline and duration assigned at submission.
- A request becomes `overdue` when its assigned Admin-review duration expires without explicit approval or rejection. Overdue never approves or rejects the request automatically.
- When a request first becomes `overdue`, the system notifies the request author once and notifies the current Building Admin. The current Building Admin then receives a reminder every 24 hours until the Admin approves, the Admin rejects, or the author withdraws. Reminder delivery follows the already-approved notification-channel rules and must not introduce a new channel.
- When a new Admin takes control of the Building, all `pending_approval` and `overdue` requests retain current state, original deadline, version history, audit history, and reminder history. The new Admin immediately receives a workload notification with counts for `pending_approval` and `overdue` requests. The reminder lifecycle is not restarted merely because the Admin changed.
- An approved ordinary vote may be cancelled by the Building Admin only before the first vote is recorded and only with a documented reason. Once the first vote is recorded, cancellation is forbidden. Cancellation must preserve the vote, request, versions, decision, cancellation reason, and audit history; it must not hard-delete the vote or let the Admin edit approved resident content.
- These ordinary request rules are separate from the dedicated Admin-replacement election workflow. They do not add current-Admin approval, cancellation, delay, or edit powers to Admin replacement.

#### 3.7.3 Voting Eligibility

- One vote is allowed per active unit.
- A voter must hold an active membership connected to an active unit.
- A user assigned to multiple units holds voting eligibility for each unit they are actively assigned to, subject to the tenant-priority rule for each unit independently.
- If a tenant is assigned to a unit, only the tenant may vote for that unit.
- If no tenant is assigned, the owner may vote.
- For ordinary Normal/Money votes, an administrator cannot vote.
- The author of an ordinary vote cannot vote on that vote.
- In an Admin-replacement ballot, both the incumbent and challenger retain their ordinary Unit-based voting rights. Their election role grants no extra vote and creates no exclusion; tenant priority and one vote per eligible Unit still apply.
- A Building's physical Unit set is fixed when the Building is created. No vote, and no occupancy change during an open vote, may create, admit, or expand a physical Unit. Quorum and the participation denominator are always Unit-based and must never be derived from resident count, membership count, occupied-Unit count, or registered-user count.
- Occupancy of an existing Unit may change while an Admin-replacement cycle is open. A previously vacant existing Unit may receive an eligible resident during the open ballot, and that newly eligible resident may cast that Unit's vote while the cycle remains nonterminal. Casting authorization always resolves against the current valid occupancy assignment; historical occupancy and vote records remain auditable.

#### 3.7.4 Result Validity

manual-close validity is:

```text
is_activated = activation_rate_snapshot >= 60
participation_rate = votes_cast / total_units_snapshot * 100
result_valid = is_activated AND participation_rate >= vote_threshold
```

When valid, normal and money votes select the option with the highest count. This is plurality among cast votes; the threshold is a participation threshold, not a winner-support threshold.

Admin-replacement behavior: `votes_cast` for a replacement cycle counts currently effective Unit votes only, excluding superseded rows, and the denominator remains the Building's fixed Unit set. After normal quorum, the majority of valid votes wins; an incumbent majority, tie, or no quorum keeps the incumbent and creates no second round. A challenger win atomically demotes the incumbent and installs the challenger, with no intermediate zero-Admin state and a complete audit record. Every terminal outcome permits a new eligible self-nomination immediately because Admin replacement has no cooldown.

Every ordinary-vote close mode, including manual close, expiry processing, and auto-close after all eligible Units vote, uses the creation-time activation and active-unit snapshots. For an Admin-replacement cycle that remains nonterminal, API-100 is the exclusive normal-result calculation/closure path and the only path that may transfer Admin, but it is invoked by server-side automation only: it runs at the earlier of the scheduled `voting_end_at` or the moment complete eligible-Unit participation is confirmed. No Admin, resident, candidate, Super Admin, or Platform Operator may call, approve, delay, or cancel that normal-result finalization. API-101 challenger withdrawal during announcement or voting and trusted challenger-ineligibility invalidation while nonterminal are separate race-locked terminal cancellation/invalidation transitions, not normal-result closure; they produce no winner or Admin transfer, preserve the incumbent and recorded votes as immutable audit history, create no cooldown, and permit immediate eligible self-nomination after terminal commit. Current active-unit changes do not alter an open vote's activation validity or participation denominator. During an open replacement ballot, authorized ordinary members see live Unit-based participation/progress only, never exact per-candidate totals; the internal authoritative tally remains exact and immediate, and finalized candidate totals may be published under the existing final-result rules. A voter can never change or withdraw their own recorded choice; the sole way a Unit's effective choice changes is the occupancy-driven supersession rule in Section 6.8D.

### 3.8 Issues, Announcements, and Notifications

#### 3.8.1 Issues

- Building community content uses one aggregate family (the canonical `issues` aggregate) with a required, server-validated resident-selected content type: `discussion` (conversation-oriented) or `issue` (a Building problem with the Q216 lifecycle). §6.8K records the governing Checkpoint-15 decisions Q211–Q225.
- An issue requires a category, title, and location scope; location scope must be `unit`, `building`, or `common_area`.
- Anonymous presentation is included in the MVP for Discussions, Issues, and comments. Anonymous hides the author from ordinary members behind the canonical localized `Anonymous` / `مجهول` label but never from the current authorized Building Admin or audit records, and the Admin surface always shows the explicit author-selected-anonymous indicator (§6.8K).
- Administrator-created issues may open directly.
- Resident-created Discussions/Issues require current Building Admin approval before any ordinary resident can discover them; rejection requires a resident-visible mandatory reason and the author may revise and resubmit the same aggregate as a new immutable revision (§6.8K).
- Approved/published Discussions and Issues may receive comments from currently active authorized Building members without per-comment preapproval.
- A comment requires text or/and media, is immutable after publication, and may be hidden (never hard-deleted) by the current Building Admin with an optional reason (§6.8K).
- For a published Issue, progress is Building-Admin-controlled and follows exactly `open -> in_progress -> resolved` (§6.8K). The legacy author/Admin close action and `closed` status are superseded for published Issues by that lifecycle; who ends/closes a published Discussion remains an explicit open product question (Q226+), so no Discussion closure action is exposed.
- Blocked members must not create or comment.
- Issue history must be retained; revisions, decisions, moderation events, and identity-presentation choices are append-only.

#### 3.8.2 Announcements

- Authorized administrators may create announcements.
- Announcements may be normal or urgent.
- All announcements require a positive duration to ends at.
- Announcements may require RSVP.
- Read state and RSVP state are tracked per profile.
- Announcements may be active or closed.
- Announcements will be visible in home screen.
- Urgent Announcements will be pined to the start of the announcement list.

#### 3.8.3 Notifications and Realtime

- Notifications are stored with portal, profile, building, event type, references, and localized message keys/parameters.
- Realtime events update feature query caches.
- Realtime channels must be scoped to authorized portal and building contexts.

- Leadership reporting is an internal platform-operations capability, not a Building Administrator or Resident feature.
- Finance / Revenue Operations owns revenue and subscription reporting; Product Leadership owns adoption, retention, and feature-usage reporting.
- During development, approved Platform Operators may use a temporary mini internal queue/reporting UI only as a transition tool.

It must not wait until post-pilot and must cover onboarding requests, internal reporting, user management, subscription management, support operations, platform-controlled configuration, trial policy configuration, pricing policy configuration, withdrawal-risk configuration, withdrawal-limit policy configuration, and withdrawal operational review.
- Reports must distinguish registered, configured, active, and paid Buildings; forecasts from realized revenue; active-unit/revenue changes; and approved feature-usage and neighborhood-density metrics.

### 3.11 Payment Aggregator and Paymob Integration

No Paymob merchant account, contract, KYC approval, SDK, API client, webhook handler, HMAC verification, order creation, payment-intention creation, SaaS billing integration, or reconciliation job is implemented in the current repository. Paymob starts from zero and is the top engineering/business priority.

The first Paymob release must include both resident-to-building payment collection and Building Suit SaaS subscription billing. They must not be split into separate launch phases unless a future approved decision changes this.

Paymob activation work must include merchant onboarding, KYC, contracting, legal/compliance validation, technical integration, callback verification, reconciliation testing, operational support procedures, and deployment/runbook readiness.

The Paymob integration must satisfy the following product requirements:

1. The backend creates the payment order or intention using a trusted amount derived from server-side charge data.
2. The client receives only the minimum redirect or payment-key material required to complete the payment.
3. The backend verifies Paymob callbacks using the required signature or HMAC mechanism.
4. Browser redirects are treated as user experience signals, not proof of payment.
5. Callback handling is idempotent and rejects duplicate or replayed settlement events.
6. The callback amount, currency, order reference, building, unit, profile, and payment status are reconciled against trusted internal records.
7. A trusted confirmed resident payment reduces the unit debt and records the building collection exactly once while marking the amount `Collected but not yet available for withdrawal` until Paymob settlement/availability confirmation.
8. Settlement availability moves the collected amount into the available balance exactly once.
9. Failed, pending, refunded, reversed, chargeback, and disputed states are represented explicitly.
10. Payment secrets and gateway credentials are stored only in secure server-side configuration.
11. Reconciliation reports identify unmatched, delayed, duplicated, reversed, charged back, or amount-mismatched transactions.
12. The final Paymob funds-separation model remains capability-gated: either separate building subaccounts/wallets or one platform account/wallet with internal per-building separation may be used only after onboarding, KYC, contracting, legal/compliance review, technical validation, and payout/reconciliation testing.
13. Resident payment fees and withdrawal fees follow the ownership and display rules in sections 3.5.3 and 3.5.5, while exact fee schedules and amounts remain Paymob-validation gated.
14. Paymob payout rails and any provider-side destination-saving mechanism remain capability-gated; Building Suit must not store full payout destination details or maintain building-owned/Admin-owned beneficiary lists.

Exact Paymob products, payment methods, payout rails, destination-saving capability, fee schedule/amounts, settlement schedule, and operational support process require clarification from engineering, finance operations, Paymob, and legal/compliance before activation. Fee ownership, no-estimated-fee gating, support-only MVP refunds, and compensating-entry reversal behavior are approved product rules.

### 5.2 E1: Portal-Isolated Identity and Access

#### E1-US1

**User Story:** As a Global Authenticated User, I want to enter an authorized portal using my shared identity so that I do not maintain separate credentials for each platform product.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- The system validates a current access token or refreshes the session once after an authentication failure.
- The backend resolves the user's profile for the trusted current portal.
- A missing portal profile returns a structured authorization or onboarding response.
- A global session without a current-portal profile does not grant portal-domain access.
- Portal resolution must not trust an arbitrary browser-supplied portal code.

#### E1-US2

**User Story:** As a Building Administrator, I want all building actions authorized against my current membership and permissions so that administrative access cannot leak across buildings.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- Every building write validates the current Building Suit profile, building membership, role, and required permission.
- Requests using a building public identifier are resolved server-side.
- A non-member request returns `403` with a structured error code.
- A request for an unknown public identifier returns `404`.
- A blocked membership is rejected for write operations.

#### E1-US3

**User Story:** As a Platform Architect, I want each portal deployed as an independent application within the monorepo so that portal releases and domain state remain isolated.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- Building Suit, Shop Suit, Business Suit, City Suit, and Super-Admin have separate application entry points and route trees.
- No portal mounts another portal's protected feature routes.
- State stores that contain domain context are portal-local.
- Server-side rendering creates state per request and does not expose protected data through public caching.
- Exact deployment domains and single-sign-on handoff require clarification from engineering.

### 5.3 E2: Building, Unit, and Membership Operations

#### E2-US1

**User Story:** As a Global Authenticated User, I want to create a residential building so that I can begin managing its units and operations.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- Location selection/confirmation happens first and is mandatory before naming/completing the building.
- Nearby building discovery appears before self-service continuation.
- Address is auto-filled from the selected location, editable, and may include an optional landmark/distinguishing note.
- The initial building name is generated from the location-derived address and remains editable.
- Building name is required and contains 2 to 100 characters.
- Total units is a positive integer no greater than 1,000.
- Country, city, latitude, and longitude are required and valid.
- A nearby-building match returns a `409` warning and requires explicit continuation.
- Successful creation creates the building, units, administrator membership, and subscription record.

#### E2-US2

**User Story:** As a Resident, I want to request membership in a specific unit so that my occupancy and building access are formally recorded.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- The request identifies the building, unit, and owner or tenant occupancy role.
- Duplicate pending or active requests are rejected with a conflict response.
- A unit may have at most one active owner and one active tenant.
- Replacement of an occupied role requires an explicit swap/replace instruction and administrator approval.

#### E2-US3

**User Story:** As a Building Administrator, I want to approve, block, remove, or transfer memberships so that building access and occupancy remain accurate.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- Only users with the relevant membership-management permission may approve or alter memberships.
- Blocking preserves permitted read-only access and rejects writes.
- Removing or transferring a member closes the relevant occupancy-history period.
- The final active administrator cannot be removed without a valid replacement process.
- Removing or transferring a resident never transfers old credit or debt to the next resident. The old balance remains tied to the old occupancy period; the new resident starts from zero.
- Registering a new Owner shows a mandatory old-obligation review before completion when old owner-related obligations exist, classifies each item as `Old Owner Debt`, `Unassigned Owner Responsibility`, or `Pre-First-Owner Unit Obligation`, and records the allowed Admin decision/audit without transferring known old Owner debt.

#### E2-US4

**User Story:** As a Global Authenticated User, I want to request that Building Suit staff create my building for me so that I do not have to enter the building and unit setup myself.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- The request captures requester contact details, the chosen location, and any known building name or unit count.
- Only a Platform Operator may claim, fulfill, cancel, or reject a request.
- Fulfillment performs the same atomic building creation as self-service creation (E2-US1) and additionally records first activation for the units described by the requester's roster.
- During pilot, the founder personally owns this assisted onboarding function, with requests claimed within 1 business day and fulfilled within 5 business days where requester information is complete.
- Rejecting a request requires a reason and does not block the requester from creating the building themselves afterward.
- The fulfilling operator and all status transitions are recorded in an audit trail.

### 5.4 E3: Transparent Finance and Subscription Control

#### E3-US1

**User Story:** As a Building Administrator, I want to create charges for units so that the building can track amounts owed.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- Charge amount is a positive number.
- The target building and unit must exist and be authorized.
- The operation requires `finance.record` and a subscription state that permits finance writes.
- A successful charge creates a receivable debit and an auditable charge record.
- Monthly generation runs automatically at the beginning of the month and can be manually generated/retried by an Admin for the approved recovery/setup cases.
- Monthly maintenance charges apply only to eligible current active occupancy Units and never to every physical Unit by default.
- Duplicate monthly generation for the same Unit, billing configuration, and month is rejected or skipped.
- Approved Special Assessment activation records total Units, active funding Units, immutable original-funder snapshot, fair share, deferred obligations, and Owner-responsibility scope; draft creation records proposals only.

#### E3-US2

**User Story:** As a Building Administrator, I want to record a resident payment so that the unit balance and available manual funds are updated transparently.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- Amount is positive.
- Method is one of `cash`, `instapay`, `wallet`, or `other`.
- The payment targets exactly one due bucket: `Monthly / Occupancy Dues`, `Owner Obligations / Special Assessments`, or an approved documented `Disputed / Under Review` flow.
- The payment creates a receivable credit exactly once.
- If amount exceeds the selected bucket's due, the excess creates the correct scoped credit: `Occupancy Credit` for current occupancy dues or `Owner Obligation Credit` for Owner/Special Assessment scope.
- Equalization Credit can be applied only when the entitlement and bucket/scope allow it, or settled through Equalization Payout, never both.
- Manual methods create a corresponding manual debit; the `wallet` method creates a corresponding wallet credit and no manual debit.
- The payment records building, unit, and profile traceability.
- The payment preview/confirmation shows selected bucket, due, applicable and excluded credits, paid amount, excess/new credit, and remaining due.

#### E3-US3

**User Story:** As a Resident, I want to view my unit's financial history so that I can understand charges, payments, and the current amount owed.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- The user must hold an authorized membership for the building and unit.
- Unit balance equals receivable debits minus receivable credits.
- A resident's personal history does not expose transactions preceding the applicable occupancy start date where the current history filter applies.
- Unit finance separates `Monthly / Occupancy Dues`, `Owner Obligations / Special Assessments`, and `Disputed / Under Review`; disputed amounts do not appear as payable due until resolved.
- Blocked-member read access follows the explicit read-only policy.
- The API returns structured errors for unauthorized or unknown unit access.

#### E3-US4

**User Story:** As a Building Administrator, I want to record expenses and withdrawals so that building funds remain auditable.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- Expense title is required and amount is positive.
- An expense creates an expense debit and manual-balance credit.
- A withdrawal creates one primary withdrawal record, reserves building balance and the applicable daily-limit amount immediately, and exposes `Pending` to authorized residents without presenting it as a completed outflow.
- The request validates available balance, the active daily-limit policy, immutable `withdrawal_limit_day`, payout destination rules, and any threshold-based step-up authentication before submission.
- No Building Admin cancellation is available after submission.
- Withdrawal state changes rely only on trusted Paymob callbacks/webhooks or documented reconciliation evidence, never browser return state.
- `Confirmed`, `Failed`, and `Reversed` primary state changes, plus `Still Pending` review-outcome events, retain detailed timeline/audit evidence without duplicating ledger rows for each state change. `Still Pending` never changes the primary status away from `Requires Review`, never releases reservations, and never posts a final outflow.

#### E3-US5

**User Story:** As a Platform Operator, I want subscription state to control building write eligibility so that unpaid or cancelled buildings become read-only without losing historical access.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- Locked and cancelled subscriptions reject all building writes with structured `403` errors while allowing sign-in and approved historical reads.
- A `past_due` subscription allows writes during the 14-day grace period. After the grace period, the subscription transitions to `locked` and the building becomes read-only.
- During trial, no subscription charge accrues. After trial, subscription charges use the approved active-unit-price and minimum-fee formula (20 EGP per active Unit, minimum 200 EGP) against the billing-cycle active-unit snapshot.
- Cancellation moves the building to read-only for 3 months and is blocked while wallet balance is positive.
- Subscription state transitions are auditable.

### 5.5 E4: Verified Payments and Reconciliation

#### E4-US1

**User Story:** As a Resident, I want to pay an eligible unit charge through Paymob so that my payment can be confirmed without manual administrator entry.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- The backend derives the payable amount and references from trusted charge data.
- The client cannot override the amount, currency, building, unit, or charge being settled.
- The backend creates and stores an internal payment attempt before redirecting to or invoking Paymob.
- If the resident chooses to overpay, the UI shows the selected bucket, due amount, paid amount, excess scoped credit, and the no-wallet/no-automatic-refund meaning before confirmation.
- The resident payment attempt targets one due bucket and shows applicable/excluded credits before confirmation; overpayment creates only the credit type allowed for that selected bucket/scope.
- If an electronic payment fee exists, the UI shows due/payment amount, fee, and total charged before confirmation; the building/unit ledger credit excludes the fee.
- A browser success redirect does not mark the payment as confirmed.
- Exact supported Paymob payment methods and fee amounts require clarification from engineering and Paymob before activation.

#### E4-US2

**User Story:** As a Payment Aggregator, I want to submit a signed payment callback so that Building Suit can settle a payment securely.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- The callback signature or HMAC is verified before processing.
- Callback order/reference, amount, currency, building, unit/profile/subscription/withdrawal context, and status are reconciled against trusted internal records.
- Duplicate callbacks are idempotent and do not duplicate ledger entries.
- Invalid, mismatched, or replayed callbacks are rejected and logged.
- A confirmed resident-payment callback reduces the resident/unit debt exactly once, marks the payment confirmed/collected, and keeps the amount out of available-for-withdrawal balance until settlement availability is confirmed.
- A settlement-availability event moves a previously collected amount into available balance exactly once.
- A confirmed withdrawal callback consumes the amount-plus-fee reservation and posts the approved withdrawal settlement effect exactly once.

#### E4-US3

**User Story:** As a Finance Operator, I want to reconcile gateway transactions against the ledger so that settlement discrepancies are detected and resolved.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- The system identifies unmatched, duplicated, delayed, failed, reversed, and amount-mismatched transactions.
- Reconciliation never edits historical ledger entries destructively.
- Corrections use approved compensating entries and retain audit context.
- Withdrawal reconciliation updates require Paymob reference or documented reconciliation evidence, reviewer, timestamp, prior state, new state, and reason/note; they are not discretionary manual confirmations.
- Reversal, refund, and chargeback results preserve the original payment and use compensating ledger operations. Building Admins and Residents do not have a direct refund action in the MVP; Platform Support / Operations handles refund cases after Paymob review and evidence validation.
- Reconciliation access is restricted to an approved operational role that is not yet defined.

### 5.6 E5: Unit-Based Governance

#### E5-US1

**User Story:** As a Resident, I want to propose a building vote so that a decision can be discussed and resolved through a structured process.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- Title, vote type, and positive duration are required.
- Duration is no more than 7 days.
- Normal and money votes contain at least two options.
- Resident-created normal and money votes enter pending approval.
- Pending and overdue resident-created ordinary vote requests are visible only to the request author and current Building Admin.
- The request author may edit or withdraw an undecided request before Admin decision; the Admin may approve or reject with a documented reason but cannot edit resident content.
- Rejected and withdrawn ordinary requests may be edited and resubmitted as the same request while preserving all prior versions, rejection reasons, withdrawn states, withdrawing actor/time, and audit history.
- Admin replacement ballots contain exactly the incumbent and one eligible self-nominated challenger after the mandatory announcement.

#### E5-US2

**User Story:** As a Unit Voter, I want to cast one vote per unit so that governance reflects unit participation.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- The vote is open and not expired.
- The voter has `vote.cast`, an active membership, and an eligible active unit.
- A user assigned to multiple units may cast one vote per eligible unit, subject to tenant-priority rules for each unit independently.
- The author cannot vote on the authored vote.
- The incumbent and challenger may vote in their Admin replacement ballot through the Units they validly represent, under the same tenant-priority and one-vote-per-Unit rules as every other eligible voter.
- Tenant priority is enforced; an owner cannot vote for a unit with an assigned tenant.
- A second result for the same unit returns a conflict; the first recorded choice is final and cannot be changed or withdrawn.
- While an Admin-replacement ballot is open, authorized ordinary members see live Unit-based participation/progress only and no exact candidate totals; internal tallies remain exact/immediate, and no resident-facing surface reveals which Unit or person selected either option.

#### E5-US3

**User Story:** As a Building Administrator, I want votes to close using explicit validity rules so that outcomes are auditable and reproducible.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- Governance activation is at least 60%.
- Participation is calculated against the total-unit snapshot.
- Normal and Admin-replacement votes require the approved 50% boundary; money votes require at least 70%.
- Admin-replacement tie/no quorum preserves the incumbent; a challenger win replaces the administrator atomically and creates an audit record.
- Manual, expiry, and automatic close use the creation-time activation, active-unit, and total-unit snapshots.

#### E5-US4

**User Story:** As a Building participant, I want ordinary vote requests and Admin transitions to preserve history and continuity so that governance work cannot disappear or be reassigned destructively.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- Approved ordinary votes can be cancelled only by the Building Admin before the first recorded vote and only with a documented reason.
- No cancellation, rejection, withdrawal, or resubmission hard-deletes the request, vote, versions, applicable rejection or cancellation reasons, withdrawn state and actor/time, or audit history.
- `pending_approval` requests move to `overdue` after the assigned review duration without automatic approval or rejection.
- The default review duration is 48 hours and can be changed only by the company-level Super Admin as one global, non-retroactive setting.
- Overdue notification is sent once to the author and every 24 hours to the current Building Admin until approval, rejection, or withdrawal.
- Admin change preserves pending/overdue request state, original deadline, version history, audit history, and reminder history; the new Admin receives counts of pending and overdue requests.
- A Building cannot pass through a zero-Admin or multiple-Admin intermediate state; an outgoing Admin cannot leave until a successor is nominated and established through an atomic transfer where applicable.
- Detailed resignation-successor mechanics beyond the no-zero-Admin invariant remain unresolved and are not implemented by this user story.

### 5.7 E6: Community Operations and Notifications

#### E6-US1

**User Story:** As a Resident, I want to submit a categorized building issue so that maintenance and operational problems are tracked outside chat threads.

**Acceptance Criteria**

- Category, title, and location scope are required.
- Location scope is `unit`, `building`, or `common_area`.

### 6.8A Checkpoint-05 approved governance and emergency-finance behavior

- A Special Assessment begins as a non-financial draft. Its preview may show project, Units, Owner responsibility, original funders, fair shares, Deferred Equalization, dates, and documents, but creates no payable obligation, debt, Equalization Credit, ledger entry, or money movement. It becomes postable only after its linked Money Vote meets the already-approved money threshold and quorum; any material change requires a new vote. Failed, expired, no-quorum, and cancelled drafts have zero financial effect.
- A Building Admin may permanently post a genuine Emergency Expense only from currently spendable Building funds. The path cannot borrow, overdraw, create Unit/Owner debt, charge members, or create a Special Assessment. Additional funding requires an urgent Money Vote.
- The Admin must submit the prescribed Emergency Expense Report within 24 hours. All authorized members are notified; a missed deadline shows `Emergency Report Overdue` without deleting or reversing the expense.
- Category is required (`safety_risk`, `main_water_leak_or_failure`, `electrical_or_fire_risk`, `dangerous_elevator_failure`, `urgent_security_issue`, `urgent_structural_damage`, or `other_emergency`); `other_emergency` needs detail, and available evidence or an evidence-unavailable reason is mandatory.
- Any active Owner/resident may submit immutable `Flag for Review / طلب مراجعة`. The Admin responds with evidence; authorized members see the review. The flag does not refund/freeze money, suspend/restrict/remove the Admin, or involve the Platform Operator as financial judge.
- Proven misuse yields `Admin Warning / إنذار إداري موثق` only after flag, response period, and successful Normal Governance Vote. A warning is same-Building history, links the full evidence chain, changes no permission, and creates no cross-Building reputation.
- Any voting-eligible active Owner/resident other than the incumbent may self-nominate without Admin approval, Platform approval, warning prerequisite, or nominee-acceptance step. The normal schedule is a fixed 24-hour announcement followed by a three-day, exactly-two-option incumbent-versus-challenger ballot using normal quorum. As corrected by Checkpoint 08, the normal result finalizes automatically at the earlier of the scheduled deadline or confirmed complete eligible-Unit participation; reached quorum or an apparent majority alone still never closes or decides it early, and no person finalizes it. Challenger withdrawal during announcement or voting, or trusted challenger-ineligibility termination while the cycle is nonterminal, is an exception that ends the schedule with no winner or Admin transfer. Both participants may vote through their eligible Units; individual choices remain secret, and while open ordinary authorized members see participation/progress only, not exact candidate totals; a voter can never change or withdraw their own recorded choice. Incumbent majority, tie, or no quorum leaves the current Admin in place; challenger majority transfers Admin atomically with no zero-Admin state. No outcome creates a cooldown, so a new eligible self-nomination may begin immediately after the cycle becomes terminal.
- All other resident-created vote requests remain `pending_approval` until the current Admin approves; rejection requires a visible audited reason and remains historical. Admin-created votes open after validation. No timeout approval, resident override, or Platform Operator approval exists.

### 6.8B Checkpoint-06 approved ordinary vote-request and Admin-continuity behavior

- Ordinary vote-request content belongs to the submitting resident until approval. Only the author may edit before approval; the Admin cannot edit resident content and may only approve or reject with a documented reason.
- Rejected and withdrawn ordinary requests may be edited and resubmitted as the same request; all versions, rejection reasons, withdrawn states, and audit history remain preserved. Rejected resubmission starts a full new review window from resubmission; Checkpoint 13 resolves withdrawn resubmission with no deadline while the original membership remains active and eligible (Section 6.8I).
- Pending, overdue, and rejected ordinary request content is visible only to the author and current Building Admin; other residents see neither pending content nor rejected history. After pre-decision withdrawal, only the author retains product-facing access for same-request editing/resubmission; the Admin loses visibility and other residents remain denied, while immutable internal audit is retained.
- The global default Admin-review duration is 48 hours, configurable only by company-level Super Admin in the platform administration area. It is global, has no Building-specific override, and applies only to new requests after the change.
- Expired review duration changes the request to `overdue` only. It never approves, rejects, opens a vote, or escalates to a Platform Operator.
- Overdue reminders are sent once to the author and every 24 hours to the current Building Admin until approval, rejection, or withdrawal, using the approved notification-channel rules.
- Approved ordinary votes may be cancelled only before the first recorded vote, only by the Building Admin, and only with a documented reason. After the first recorded vote, cancellation is forbidden.
- Vote requests belong to the Building. Admin changes preserve pending/overdue state, deadlines, version history, audit history, and reminder history; the new Admin is notified immediately of pending and overdue counts.
- A Building must always have exactly one active Admin. Resignation or handover cannot complete as an isolated action that leaves no Admin; the outgoing Admin must nominate a successor before leaving, and any applicable transition must be atomic with no zero-Admin or multiple-Admin intermediate state. Detailed resignation-successor mechanics beyond that invariant are not answered here.

### 6.8C Checkpoint-07 approved Admin-election behavior

- Replacement uses self-nomination only. No person can nominate another, no nominee-acceptance step exists, and neither the current Admin nor a Platform role approves the election.
- Exactly one active cycle and one challenger may exist per Building. The normal schedule remains a fixed 24-hour announcement followed by a three-day ballot. Checkpoint 08 narrows the Checkpoint-07 wording: reached quorum or an apparent majority still never closes or decides the ballot early, but confirmed complete eligible-Unit participation now finalizes it automatically (see Section 6.8D). Challenger withdrawal during announcement or voting, or trusted challenger-ineligibility termination while the cycle is nonterminal, is an approved terminal exception and produces no winner or Admin transfer.
- The incumbent and challenger retain normal Unit-based voting eligibility; neither receives an extra vote. Ballots are secret at Unit/person level. While the ballot is open, authorized ordinary members see live participation/progress only and no exact per-candidate totals; authoritative internal tallies remain exact and immediate, and finalized totals follow the existing final-result publication rules.
- A recorded election choice is final for the voter who cast it: no voter may change or withdraw their own choice. Checkpoint 08 adds the single narrow exception in Section 6.8D, where a newly eligible resident of the same Unit commits a replacement vote that supersedes the Unit's previous effective vote.
- The challenger may withdraw during either `announcement` or `voting` while the cycle is nonterminal. Withdrawal terminates it as `cancelled_challenger_withdrawn`; the incumbent remains, any recorded votes remain immutable audit history but produce no winner or Admin transfer, and the current Admin cannot block or decide the withdrawal.
- Incumbent majority, tie, no quorum, or challenger ineligibility produces no new Admin. A challenger majority transfers the role atomically and auditably. Withdrawal/open/close races are locked so exactly one terminal result commits.
- Admin replacement has no cooldown after any terminal outcome. A new eligible self-nomination may start immediately when no cycle is active.
- Challenger eligibility must remain valid until the result. An ineligible challenger cannot win: the incumbent remains, no cooldown applies, and a new eligible self-nomination may start immediately after termination. The trusted event trigger and execution timing are a **Derived Technical Open Item** owned by Engineering and Security under ARCH-OPEN-12/DB-OPEN-14/SEC-ADR-08, not a new business decision; the current Admin must never control that determination.

### 6.8D Checkpoint-08 approved replacement completion, occupancy, and vote-supersession behavior

This section implements the locked decisions Q106, Q109, Q110, Q117, and corrected Q119. It narrows Checkpoint-07 wording only where stated and leaves every other Checkpoint-07 rule intact.

**Automatic normal-result completion (Q106, Q109)**

- The Admin-replacement normal result is finalized automatically at the earlier of the scheduled voting deadline or confirmed complete eligible-Unit participation. Reaching full eligible-Unit participation is the only additional early normal-result completion condition introduced by this checkpoint.
- No Admin, resident, candidate, Super Admin, or Platform Operator has any action that finalizes, closes, approves, delays, or cancels the normal result. There is no manual `Close election` or `Approve result` capability anywhere in the product.
- Reached quorum alone and an apparent majority alone still never close or decide the ballot early.
- The backend re-evaluates completion automatically after every successful vote-affecting transaction, and at scheduled deadline processing. The evaluation is server-authoritative, transactional, concurrency-safe, idempotent, and auditable. The client never calculates or commits the authoritative result.
- Withdrawal and trusted challenger-ineligibility remain separate terminal exception paths unchanged from Checkpoint 07. Neither produces a winner or transfers the Admin role.

**Meaning of complete participation**

Complete participation is never `number_of_vote_rows == total_units`. It means every Unit that is currently eligible to submit an effective vote already holds one, and no eligible Unit can still cast a first effective vote. The evaluation must distinguish historical vote rows, superseded vote rows, currently effective Unit votes, Units currently eligible to submit an effective vote, the fixed Unit denominator, and Units that currently have no eligible voter and therefore cannot cast.

**Fixed Units and occupancy change (Q117)**

- The Building's physical Unit set is fixed independently of voting. An open vote never creates or admits a newly invented Unit, and the physical denominator never grows because a resident joined.
- An existing Unit may change occupancy while a cycle is open, including a vacant Unit receiving an eligible resident. That newly eligible resident may cast that existing Unit's vote while the cycle remains nonterminal.
- Quorum and the denominator stay Unit-based and are never resident-count-based, membership-count-based, occupied-Unit-only, or registered-user-based.

**Vote supersession within the same Unit (corrected Q119)**

- A previous effective Unit vote is not invalidated merely because the Unit's resident changed, a new tenant entered, a former voter lost occupancy, or an occupancy assignment was updated. Until a newly eligible resident actually casts a replacement vote, the previous effective Unit vote remains counted.
- When the newly eligible resident successfully commits a valid replacement vote while the cycle is nonterminal, that single atomic transaction marks the previous Unit vote superseded, stops it contributing to live and final totals, and makes the new vote the single effective vote for the Unit. There is never more than one simultaneously effective vote for a Unit.
- The earlier vote is never physically deleted. It is preserved as immutable audit history with explicit supersession metadata: which vote superseded it, when, and on what eligibility basis.
- A failed, rejected, or rolled-back replacement attempt leaves the previous vote effective and totals unchanged.
- Secrecy is preserved against direct disclosure. Normal residents must not learn which person or Unit chose which candidate merely because a vote was superseded, and no interface announces that a specific Unit's vote was replaced.
- **Resolved publication rule (Checkpoint 09; closes DEC-16).** While the replacement ballot is open, authorized ordinary members receive Unit-based participation/progress only and no exact per-candidate totals. The authoritative internal tally remains exact and immediate, and finalized candidate totals may be exposed under the existing final-result rules. Because an isolated supersession produces no public per-candidate −1/+1 delta, the publication projection must not emit option totals, supersession events, per-Unit timestamps, or another choice-revealing signal while open.

**Cycle identity and automatic retry recognition (Q110)**

- A replacement cycle has no resident-authored free-text title. Its identity is the current incumbent Admin versus the eligible resident who self-nominated as challenger, and every displayed label or summary is generated by the system from those identities.
- If a later cycle is started by the same challenger against the same still-serving incumbent in the same Building after a previous cycle became terminal, the system recognizes it as a retry automatically. Retry recognition is derived from historical cycle data, never from a typed title, an Admin decision, a checkbox, or a user-selected retry flag.
- A retry is a new immutable cycle with its own timestamps, snapshots, votes, result, and audit history. It never modifies or reopens the previous terminal cycle.

### 6.8E Checkpoint-09 approved request and financial-governance behavior

This section records Q121–Q135. The replacement-ballot publication paragraph is a separate carry-forward clarification and is not an additional numbered question.

**Ordinary requests (Q121–Q122)**

- Pre-decision withdrawal leaves product-facing content/history visible only to the author. The current Admin loses access, other residents have none, and immutable internal audit retention is neither hard deletion nor Admin-facing visibility. The author may edit and resubmit the same withdrawn request.
- Resubmitting the same rejected request starts a full new Admin-review window at the resubmission timestamp. Previous elapsed or remaining time never carries forward; rejected versions, reasons, timestamps, and audit remain immutable. Checkpoint 13 later resolves withdrawn-request resubmission: no resubmission deadline exists while the original membership remains active and eligible (Section 6.8I).

**Money Vote structure and authorization (Q123–Q129)**

- Every Money Vote carries a typed server-owned topic. For `spending_authorization`, one spend proposal plus no-spend or multiple spend alternatives plus no-spend remain required: each spend option has a positive structured amount and the single required no-spend option has no positive amount. The separate `no_vote_limit_change` topic follows the fixed policy ballot below and is not a spending-authorization shape. Existing quorum, eligibility, and participation rules do not change.
- A successful `spending_authorization` Money Vote with a spend winner creates only financial authorization. It performs no reservation, debit, expense, payable, ledger posting, or proof of spend. Special Assessment remains a dedicated draft/vote/activation workflow; only its separate unchanged-successful-vote activation creates the already-approved financial effects.
- The `spending_authorization` creator selects a disclosed percentage tolerance, defaulting to 0%, up to a versioned and audited company Super-Admin global maximum. Before voting, every voter sees each option's base amount, the tolerance percentage, and calculated maximum exposure. Tolerance becomes immutable when voting begins. The exact global maximum is unresolved.
- The `spending_authorization` creator selects a separate execution-validity duration up to a versioned company maximum, visible before voting and immutable after voting starts. If no real documented financial commitment begins execution before expiry, execution cannot begin and a new Money Vote is required. A user-set `Started` flag alone is insufficient. A confirmed order, binding contract/invoice/order commitment, deposit/down payment, or equivalent auditable commitment before expiry permits later completion/installments under the same purpose and ceiling. The exact global maximum duration is unresolved.
- Every Expense that consumes authorization must link the relevant Money Vote at posting. The server atomically validates Building scope, successful result and winning option, purpose relationship, validity or qualifying pre-expiry commitment, cumulative consumed amount, tolerance ceiling, ledger invariants, idempotency, and concurrency. One authorization may cover multiple same-purpose expenses/installments; cumulative effective posted operations cannot exceed the maximum authorized amount. Reversals use existing compensating-ledger semantics, and no new refund workflow is introduced.

**No-vote spending governance (Q130–Q135)**

- Outside Emergency Expense, the Building Admin may spend without Money Vote only within the Building's effective no-vote spending limit, evaluated cumulatively per underlying purpose/project. Splitting rows cannot circumvent the threshold. Explicit same-purpose grouping and aggregation are server-authoritative and concurrency-safe; supporting risk signals are auditable, while exact fuzzy thresholds remain an Engineering/Security design item.
- Company Super Admin controls a versioned no-vote-limit policy containing the platform default initial limit and platform maximum/boundary. A new Building snapshots the applicable default and policy version at creation; the numeric values remain unresolved. This aggregate alone owns new-Building-only applicability and exceptional legacy propagation. Separately, a versioned Money Vote-constraint policy owns maximum tolerance and maximum execution-validity duration; a new version applies to newly created/opened Money Votes across existing and new Buildings from its effective time, never retroactively changes an open/closed Vote, and accepts no Building-propagation mode.
- After initialization, only a valid same-Building `no_vote_limit_change` Money Vote whose server-derived `approve_proposed_limit` option wins may change that Building's effective value. The ballot snapshots the current value/provenance/Building policy version and applicable maximum/version; proposes one different in-policy EGP value; has exactly the fixed approve/keep choices; and has no tolerance, validity, spending purpose, exposure, or financial authorization. Building Admin has no direct mutation path.
- API-046 is the sole application boundary. On a valid approval win it locks the Vote, Building policy row, and policy snapshots; revalidates topic/proposal/result/scope/maximum; closes and applies exactly once; records old/new values and provenance, source Vote, policy versions, eligibility basis, actor/system transition, timestamps, immutable audit and outbox; sets provenance to `building_money_vote`; and protects the Building from later exceptional propagation. It creates no Expense, payable, reservation, Special Assessment, financial authorization, balance movement, or ledger entry. `keep_current_limit`, tie, no quorum, expiry, cancellation, and failure close without changing value, provenance, or protection; retries/concurrent closes cannot apply twice.
- A normal later platform-default change applies to new Buildings only. Existing Buildings keep their creation snapshot unless a valid local `approve_proposed_limit` result is applied or an explicitly exceptional policy change is propagated under the eligibility rule below.
- Super Admin may explicitly mark a particular policy change for exceptional propagation to eligible older Buildings. Eligibility is limited to Buildings that have never had a valid local `no_vote_limit_change` ballot in which `approve_proposed_limit` won and was applied. Only that API-046-applied approve-proposed result protects the Building; keep-current and every other non-approval terminal outcome do not. Policy version, applicability, exceptional propagation, creation snapshot, local-vote protection, and effective-value provenance (`platform_creation_snapshot`, `platform_propagated_policy`, or `building_money_vote`, or equivalent) remain distinct and audited.
- Exceptional legacy propagation is scheduled and notified under the Checkpoint-10 contract in §6.8F; it is never an immediate or silent overwrite.

**Admin-replacement open-ballot publication (carry-forward clarification)**

- During an open replacement ballot, ordinary authorized members see participation/progress only and never live exact per-candidate totals. Internal tallies remain exact and immediate; finalized candidate totals may be published under the existing final-result rules. Pending approval, rejected revision, resubmission/review, and reopen-request states remain explicit workflow states/events and are never confused with the three published progress states. - Only the current Building Admin passing the live canonical authorization predicate may move a published Issue `open -> in_progress` or `in_progress -> resolved`. Every transition records immutable acting-Admin attribution, live authority context, prior/next state, server time, lifecycle version, and correlation/audit evidence; authority is reauthorized inside the transaction, so a former/replaced Admin or stale cached client cannot commit. The author and other residents cannot directly set progress state, and the Admin cannot edit the resident's Issue body while changing status.
- When resolving an Issue, the Admin may supply a safe resolution summary, but it is not required. A supplied summary is normalized, bounded, sanitized, and stored immutably with the resolution transition; its absence never blocks an otherwise valid resolution, UI wording never shows a required marker or validation error for an omitted summary, and an empty summary is never read as "no action occurred" — the immutable status transition remains authoritative.
- After an Issue becomes `resolved`, its immutable original author may request reopening/relisting exactly once for that Issue aggregate, with a normalized, nonblank, non-placeholder reason explaining why the problem remains unresolved. The request is not a direct state change and does not immediately republish/reopen the Issue; it enters Building Admin review while the prior Issue, progress history, optional resolution summary, comments, and identity-presentation history are retained. A second request is rejected as exhausted; idempotent replay does not consume an additional attempt. No new unrelated Issue, company escalation, Vote, deadline, or automatic reopen exists.
- The current authorized Building Admin may accept or reject the one reopening/relisting request; both outcomes require a normalized, nonblank, non-placeholder Admin reason visible safely to the author. Rejection preserves the resolved Issue and records the reason immutably. Acceptance reopens the same aggregate to `open` and publishes the approved new revision/presentation per Q224–Q225, retaining all earlier chronology. The Admin cannot edit the author's reopening reason, revised content, or presentation choice; competing accept/reject/status/Admin-replacement commands resolve through first-valid-atomic-commit-wins and create no losing partial event/notification/cache artifact.

**Comments and moderation (Q221–Q222)**

- Every currently active authorized member of the same Building may comment on an approved/published Discussion or Issue. Comments publish without a separate per-comment Building Admin preapproval step (option C was explicitly rejected); creation requires live membership, approved-parent/version, and Building/content visibility authorization inside the transaction. Text and/or canonically authorized media is required: optional `file_public_ids` is already supported, revalidated for same-Building scope, uploader/attachment authority, completed upload, safety, and private access, and linked immutably on commit. Former members, pending applicants, other Buildings, company staff, and unauthorized accounts cannot add comments or discover concealed content. Comments are immutable resident-authored entries after publication and the Building Admin cannot edit them. Anonymous media carries no identity-bearing filename/path/URL/descriptor/signed-access/telemetry/download signal; hiding conceals its media from ordinary residents while preserving existing authorized Admin/audit links. Reactions, threaded replies, mentions, attachment-policy extensions beyond canonical rules, edit windows, deletion, pinning, sorting options, and notification policy are not inferred unless already canonically approved elsewhere.
- The current authorized Building Admin may hide an inappropriate published comment. A hide reason is optional and its absence never blocks the action; a supplied reason is normalized, bounded, sanitized, and retained with the immutable moderation event. Hiding never deletes, overwrites, anonymizes, or detaches the original comment, author attribution, anonymity selection, or history. Ordinary resident surfaces replace hidden content with a neutral non-identifying unavailable/hidden presentation and never leak the optional reason unless a canonical safe product rule already explicitly allows it. No restore, appeal, self-delete, auto-hide, mass moderation, or company escalation behavior exists; current Admin authority is reauthorized at commit time and concurrent hide/retry is idempotent and race-safe.

**Anonymous presentation and identity immutability (Q223–Q225)**

- At initial submission of a Discussion/Issue and at creation of a comment, the author explicitly chooses named or anonymous presentation. For anonymous content, ordinary residents see only the canonical localized `Anonymous` / `مجهول` presentation and never receive the author's real name, avatar, Unit, profile public ID, contact data, or correlatable identity metadata through any surface. The current authorized Building Admin sees the immutable real author identity for approval/moderation/accountability, and the Admin UI always shows a clear `author selected anonymous` badge/state so the Admin neither treats the content as named nor exposes the identity to residents. Anonymous presentation is not anonymous storage: immutable server ownership, authorization, abuse controls, and protected audit attribution remain. Anonymous-author identity is never exposed to other residents through feeds, detail, comments, notifications, realtime, search, exports, analytics, logs, caches, error payloads, cursor ordering, filenames, URLs, moderation placeholders, or timing/count side channels, and no cross-post pseudonym or stable anonymous alias lets residents correlate the author's separate submissions.
- The author cannot toggle a submitted/published revision between named and anonymous presentation in place. A pending submitted revision keeps the presentation choice it had when submitted; editing/resubmission creates the next immutable revision. A published Discussion/Issue keeps its publication revision's identity presentation for its entire lifecycle and historical rendering, and a comment's choice becomes final at creation because comments have no approved resubmission/relisting lifecycle. A permitted rejected-content resubmission or resolved-Issue reopening/relisting may choose named or anonymous presentation afresh for the new revision, affecting only that newly approved publication revision; older revisions, decisions, comments, timelines, and audit history retain their original presentation and are never rewritten.
- A rejected Discussion/Issue resubmission and the one resolved-Issue reopening/relisting request remain non-public until the current Building Admin reviews the new revision, which is a new immutable revision inside the same aggregate — never a new unrelated record or an in-place mutation. On approval, ordinary residents see only the newly approved current revision with its selected presentation while authorized chronology retains prior revisions and identity-presentation states under the correct visibility rules; on rejection, the new revision and mandatory Admin rejection reason are retained without changing the previously published historical revision. For a resolved Issue, Q219's one-request limit and Q220's mandatory reasons govern this review.

**Server authority, tenancy, and concurrency**

- All authorization, state transitions, revisions, moderation actions, and concurrency behavior are server-authoritative, tenant-isolated, auditable, and race-safe: canonical server time, expected monotonic lifecycle versions, deterministic lock ordering, scoped idempotency fingerprints, one atomic transaction per command, and first-valid-atomic-commit-wins with no losing partial state, event, notification, realtime, search, or cache artifact. The server always derives actor, role, time, author identity, lifecycle version, and presentation; the client cannot supply a different author, Admin-visible identity, Building scope, moderation state, approval state, progress state, or resident projection.

**Open boundary**

- Who ends/closes an approved Discussion and its exact lifecycle, ended-Discussion reopening beyond the approved fresh-review principle, Discussion closure reasons/limits, categories beyond the canonical existing category/reference-data contract, Issue priority/assignment/scheduling/deadlines/reminders/escalation/SLA, reactions/threaded replies/mentions/comment editing/self-deletion/restore/moderation appeal, report-abuse workflow beyond Admin hide, notification recipients/channels/timing, post-membership historical visibility and retention, numeric content/attachment/pagination/rate/retention thresholds and future media-policy extensions, creation/relisting after terminal history beyond the one approved reopening request, and every Q226+ topic remain open and are not inferred; optional canonical comment media itself is not an open feature. Ambiguous security or mutation questions fail closed. Ordinary Building topics/issues remain internal to the exact Building; the Building Admin is the only moderation/approval actor, and no company/Super Admin content-review queue, company adjudication/escalation, employee moderation permission, public/global community content, or cross-Building feed/discovery/search/analytics/identity correlation/reputation exists.
