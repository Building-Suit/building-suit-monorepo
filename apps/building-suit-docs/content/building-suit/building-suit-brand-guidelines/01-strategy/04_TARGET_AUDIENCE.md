# Target Audience

> **Status:** Complete (initial). The people Building Suit is designed for and how the brand speaks to each. Grounded in the MVP product roles (admin, resident, owner, tenant) and the brand promise of *clarity you can trust*. Service-provider scope is Future/Post-MVP only.

---

## Primary audience
**Self-managed residential buildings** — and within them, the two roles that drive adoption:

1. **Building administrators / committee members** — the people responsible for collecting charges, paying for shared expenses, and keeping order. They feel the pain most and are the ones who bring a building onto the platform.
2. **Residents (owners and tenants)** — the people who pay charges, want visibility, raise issues, and vote. Their trust and everyday use make the platform stick.

**Shared context:** mobile-first users, mixed tech literacy, primarily Arabic-speaking with English support (RTL-aware), in markets where building management is informal, cash-heavy, and dispute-prone.

---

## Secondary audience
- **Building owners / investors** who don't live on-site but want oversight of finances and decisions.
- **Future/Post-MVP service providers** only if provider scope is separately reapproved; they are not an MVP audience.
- **The platform operator / future portals** — stakeholders for the wider Suit ecosystem (Shop, Business, City Suit) who care that the brand reads as a trustworthy, extensible platform.

---

## Admin users
**Who they are:** the building's organiser — often a volunteer committee head or a designated manager who currently runs everything via chat, notebook, and cash.

**Goals:**
- Collect monthly charges and reconcile who has paid.
- Record and justify shared expenses transparently.
- Manage residents, units, owners/tenants, approvals, and blocking.
- Communicate (announcements, urgent notices) and resolve issues.
- Run fair votes and, eventually, hand over the role cleanly.

**Frustrations today:** chasing payments, disputes over money, everything depending on them, no clean handover, lost records.

**How the brand serves them:** position Building Suit as the tool that makes them **trusted and unburdened** — "your records are clean, your building can see them, and you're no longer the single point of failure."

---

## Residents
**Who they are:** owners and tenants living in the building who pay charges and want to know their building is run fairly.

**Goals:**
- See their unit balance and what they owe and why.
- See where the building's money goes.
- Raise issues and get them resolved.
- Stay informed (announcements) and have a say (votes).

**Frustrations today:** no visibility into finances, decisions made without them, important news lost in group chats, uncertainty about whether their money is handled properly.

**How the brand serves them:** lead with **transparency and fairness** — "see exactly where your money goes and have a real say." Keep copy and UI simple and warm so every resident, regardless of tech skill, feels confident.

> **Owner vs. tenant nuance:** the product models occupancy explicitly, with tenant-priority voting where a tenant is assigned. Copy must use "owner" and "tenant" precisely, and never imply tenants are second-class — both are full residents of the community.

---

## Building owners
**Who they are:** owners (sometimes absentee/investor owners) who may not occupy the unit but carry financial and governance stakes.

**Goals:**
- Oversight of building finances and their unit's balance from anywhere.
- Confidence that charges and expenses are legitimate and recorded.
- Participation in ownership-level decisions and elections.

**Frustrations today:** being out of the loop, relying on word of mouth, no trustworthy remote view.

**How the brand serves them:** emphasise **remote transparency and accountability** — "oversee your building's money and decisions from your phone, wherever you are." Premium, financial-grade visual trust signals matter most to this group.

---

## Future/Post-MVP service providers
**Who they are:** local tradespeople and maintenance businesses who could become a future audience only if provider scope is separately reapproved after MVP.

**Goals:**
- Future-only: be discoverable by nearby buildings with a credible profile.
- Future-only: be contacted for issues and maintenance.

**Frustrations today:** rely on informal referrals; no structured way to reach buildings.

**How the brand serves them:** no active MVP messaging, navigation, profile, directory, or contact journey. If reapproved later, present a **simple, credible directory presence** through a separate Future/Post-MVP scope decision.

---

## Stakeholder pain points
A consolidated view of what the brand is solving for, by stakeholder:

| Stakeholder | Core pain |
|---|---|
| **Admins** | Manual collection, money disputes, single-person dependency, no clean handover |
| **Residents** | No financial visibility, decisions made without them, news lost in chats |
| **Owners** | No trustworthy remote oversight; out of the loop on money and decisions |
| **Tenants** | Unclear rights/visibility; risk of being sidelined in decisions |
| **Future/Post-MVP service providers** | Hard to be discovered; dependent on informal referrals; not an MVP audience |
| **Platform operator** | Needs a brand that signals trust and can scale across future portals |

## Emotional needs
Beneath the functional needs, the brand must satisfy deeper feelings:

- **Trust** — "I believe my building's money is handled honestly."
- **Peace of mind** — "I don't have to worry or chase; it's all recorded."
- **Fairness** — "Everyone is treated by the same clear rules."
- **Belonging** — "I'm part of a community that runs well together."
- **Pride / dignity** — "My building feels well-managed and respectable."
- **Relief (admins)** — "I'm no longer carrying this alone."

The logo's warm, lit windows and solid navy form are designed to evoke exactly these feelings: safety, warmth, belonging, and dependable order.

---

## Product expectations
What these audiences expect from Building Suit — and what the brand must therefore deliver consistently:

- **Mobile-first and fast.** Everything works well on a phone, on the move.
- **Simple enough for everyone.** Usable across all tech-literacy levels; nothing intimidating.
- **Bilingual and localised.** Natural Arabic and English, full RTL support, locally appropriate money/date formats.
- **Trustworthy with money.** Accurate balances, auditable history, honest corrections — visibly reliable.
- **Transparent by default.** Residents can see what they're entitled to see, without asking.
- **Calm and clear in hard moments.** Errors, overdue balances, and urgent notices handled with composure.
- **Premium but approachable.** Looks polished and credible, yet welcoming to non-technical residents.
- **Reliable and continuous.** The building's history persists and survives administrator handover.
