## Proposed claim flow

1. Verify the current patient product/environment Auth user and session through the server. A patient portal resolver must be server-owned and separate from doctor/secretary context. User-editable metadata never supplies its role. The future patient client cannot reuse the clinician app's credential namespace or silently take its session.
2. Accept an opaque clinic-issued relationship reference, not a phone/email search or global patient picker. Before verification, responses reveal no patient name, chart existence, other practice, diagnosis or relationship list. An invitation or OTP alone proves neither identity nor legal authority.
3. Issue a random, purpose-bound, single-use challenge whose hash is stored server-side with authenticated subject/session, doctor/practice/relationship, policy revision and command identity. Proposed technical bounds are256-bit entropy,10minute expiry and at most5 failed attempts; these are reviewable proposals, not deployed settings. Use server time and no raw token/private identifiers in URLs, logs or analytics.
4. Require clinic-assisted evidence review by a separately authorized reviewer, bound to the same subject/relationship/challenge and exact policy version. The accountable policy must select acceptable assurance methods and reviewer authority. Government-ID images/numbers and biometric collection are not authorized; there is no evidence that a provider or legal basis is approved. Administrative patient/queue/contact rights cannot substitute for a new reviewed claim-management capability.
5. Show the patient the exact practice/relationship scope and purpose/withdrawal terms, then record explicit consent for that scope/version. A successful login, claim or caregiver invitation never enables medication reminders, marketing, messaging or reciprocal doctor access. The requested affirmative action must be separate from identity evidence.
6. Atomically consume the challenge and create the single scoped association/consent/audit receipt. Enforce expected versions, unique consumption and idempotent acknowledgement. An uncertain response is not success and must never create a new claim key automatically. Two concurrent devices cannot consume/rebind the same challenge or turn another subject's receipt into authority.
7. Resolve every subsequent read/command from current server subject/session, association, relationship, purpose consent and caregiver grants. Client portal flags, contact equality, cached tokens and UI navigation do not grant access. Account, relationship or session changes clear private memory and suppress late results. No private offline cache or offline signing is approved.

Proposed states are `challenge_pending → evidence_review → consent_pending → active`, with expiry/denial/dispute/revocation branches. These states describe a candidate design; `active` is not implemented or available until the genuine review and later source/runtime obligations pass. No existing clinical release, record, channel or consent is silently upgraded into a claim.

## Withdrawal, dispute and recovery

Withdrawal revokes the app association/purpose consent and any dependent delegation for that scope, invalidates server grant versions and pending delivery eligibility, and clears private app state on revalidation. It does not erase the clinic's immutable clinical/audit record or decide legal retention. Contact-consent withdrawal remains a separate operation; clinical/legal retention and privacy requests require their own reviewed policy.

A dispute quarantines the contested association immediately. No chart reads, reminder scheduling, caregiver delegation or recovery transfer proceeds while it is disputed. Other doctors' independent relationships and the authenticated account remain separate. Every resolution requires attributable evidence and a reason; no contact-based merge or support-agent override is proposed.

Account recovery restores authentication only. Rebinding a relationship after lost credentials or identity dispute requires fresh claim review, revokes superseded grants/sessions and records the previous association's disposition. Email/SMS recovery, reused invitation codes and matching household contacts cannot transfer a chart or caregiver authority.

Caregiver capacity, dependency age/legal basis, verification evidence, permitted views, expiry and withdrawal require accountable clinical/privacy/legal review in M5-003. M3's synthetic communication guardian flag cannot satisfy them. Separate grants must prevent cross-dependent, cross-practice and cross-doctor leakage; a caregiver is never silently treated as the patient Auth subject.
